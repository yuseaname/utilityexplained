## Turn Your Numbers Into Dollars

Reading about rates is step one; step two is applying them to *your* household. These calculators take the watts, hours, and rates off your own bills and meters and return the dollar figures — no guessing, no national averages pretending to be your bill.

Each calculator explains its math as it goes, so you can check every step against your own utility's numbers:

1. **[Electricity cost calculator](/calculators/electricity-cost/)** — what any appliance costs to run, from a phone charger to a heat pump, at your rate or the national average.
2. More tools ship as they're verified — every calculator on this site shows its formula and sources on the page.

If you're not sure which number from your bill to plug in, start with [how to read your electric bill](/blog/how-to-read-your-electric-bill/) or decode your water bill's units in [what CCF means on a water bill](/blog/what-is-ccf-on-a-water-bill/).
