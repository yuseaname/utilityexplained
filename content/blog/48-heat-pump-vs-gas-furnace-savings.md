## Quick Answer

**A heat pump and a gas furnace should be compared from a specific home’s heating and cooling loads, installed equipment proposal, utility tariffs, fuel availability, operating requirements, and local program terms.** Do not assume a universal savings result, break-even period, climate winner, tax-credit amount, or equipment lifespan.

DOE describes heat pumps as equipment that moves heat and notes that several types are available. The agency also emphasizes proper sizing from accurate heating and cooling loads.[1][2]

## Start With a Load Calculation

Ask a qualified HVAC professional for a documented heating and cooling load calculation, not a recommendation based only on floor area or the capacity of the existing equipment. DOE Building Science Education material says right-sizing begins with determining the home's heating and cooling loads.[2]

Provide information about insulation, windows, ducts, air leakage, occupancy, equipment location, and any comfort problems. The written scope should identify the design assumptions and the equipment proposed.

## Compare the Equipment and Installation Scope

Use the same written scope for each option:

| Decision item | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Equipment | Exact model, fuel, ratings, capacity, and manufacturer documentation |
| Home work | Duct, electrical, gas, ventilation, condensate, control, and panel work |
| Comfort design | Heating and cooling loads, zones, backup approach, and commissioning |
| Operating cost | Current gas and electric tariffs, billing components, and expected use |
| Maintenance | Owner tasks, service requirements, and warranty conditions |
| Incentives | Eligibility, timing, documentation, and current program terms |

ENERGY STAR explains that AFUE is an efficiency rating for gas heating equipment. Use the exact ratings and manufacturer documentation for the proposed models, rather than comparing general labels alone.[3]

## Review Costs With Your Actual Rates

Ask each contractor to identify assumptions behind any operating-cost estimate. Verify the rate plan, delivery and supply charges, fixed charges, billing period, and expected fuel or electricity use against your own provider's current documents. A lower equipment quote or one predicted operating cost is not by itself a complete comparison.

If a tax credit or rebate is mentioned, confirm the program administrator, qualifying equipment, deadline, required installation documentation, and whether the offer can be combined with other programs before signing a contract.

## Questions Before Signing

1. What load calculation and design conditions support this equipment size?
2. What work is included for ducts, electrical service, gas, controls, and permits?
3. Which equipment ratings and operating assumptions are used in the proposal?
4. What maintenance and commissioning steps are required for warranty and safe operation?
5. Which current incentives, if any, are documented as applicable to this installation?
