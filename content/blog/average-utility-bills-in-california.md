## Quick Answer: What Utilities Cost in California {#quick-answer}

**Electricity:** the California residential rate averaged **29.75¢/kWh in 2026** (EIA preliminary year-to-date; 30.92¢ final for 2025) — above the 18.16¢/kWh national average. At the EIA national-standard 899 kWh month, that is **$267/month** for electricity.

**Natural gas:** California residential gas averaged **$22.01/thousand cubic feet in 2025** (EIA annual) — about **$2.12/therm** after conversion. A standardized 75-therm heating month runs **$159**.

**Water & sewer:** there is **no reliable state average** — water is priced city-by-city in California. Nationally the combined bill runs about $70–$90/month; see [what CCF means on a water bill](/blog/what-is-ccf-on-a-water-bill/) to decode yours.

## Why California rates look like this {#why-california-rates-look-like-this}

California's electric rate sits above the 18.16¢/kWh national average — among the higher-rate states. Market structure matters as much as the number: California runs regulated, with tiered and time-of-use rate designs on the large investor-owned utilities. That means your rate is set through your utility and the state regulator — the levers that move your bill are usage and rate design (tiers, time-of-use), not supplier shopping. [Which rate plan fits your household](/blog/tiered-electricity-rates-explained/) sorts the options.

The EIA trend matters too: California's residential rate moved from 30.92¢/kWh (2025 final) to 29.75¢/kWh (2026 to date), a 3.8% decrease. On the gas side, residential prices went from $19.14/Mcf in 2024 to $22.01/Mcf in 2025 — a 15% increase. And because the cost driver here is a wide spread between mild coastal and extreme inland climate zones, your own bill swings more month-to-month than any annual average suggests. For how the pieces fit together, see [how utility rates, fees, and usage work together](/blog/how-utility-rates-fees-usage-work-together/).

## Water and sewer in California {#water-and-sewer-in-california}

Water and sewer are set by your city or water district, not the state — two neighbors across a city line can pay very different rates for the same usage. Instead of a state average, run this check:

1. **Find the unit on your bill** — most water bills measure in CCF (hundred cubic feet). [What CCF means on a water bill](/blog/what-is-ccf-on-a-water-bill/) decodes it in two minutes.
2. **Benchmark usage** — a typical household runs about 2–4 CCF per person per month; well above that, a leak is the usual suspect ([the 10-minute check](/blog/who-responsible-water-leak-before-after-meter/)).
3. **Check fixed charges** — many water systems have raised base charges faster than usage rates; your bill can climb even with flat usage. [Why your water bill is high](/blog/06-water-bill-too-high/) walks the whole statement.

{{< faq "What is the average gas bill in California?">}}

California residential gas averaged $22.01 per thousand cubic feet in 2025 (EIA) — roughly $2.12 per therm. A standardized 75-therm heating month runs about $159, but gas bills are seasonal: low-use summer months often run $25–$40, while winter peaks far higher. [Why gas bills spike in winter](/blog/07-gas-bill-too-high/) explains the swing.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is there an average water bill for California?">}}

No reliable one — water and sewer rates in California are set city-by-city or by water district, so a single state number would be fiction. Compare your bill's CCF usage against the 2–4 CCF per person per month benchmark, and read [why your water bill is high](/blog/06-water-bill-too-high/) if you're above it.
