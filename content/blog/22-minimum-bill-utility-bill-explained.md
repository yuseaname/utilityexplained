## Quick Answer

**A minimum bill is the least you owe for service in a billing period no matter how little you use — if usage charges fall below the tariff's stated floor, the floor is what you pay.** Rate schedules write it as a "Minimum Charge" or "minimum monthly charge."

Decision rule: before calling a tiny-usage bill a billing error, check the rate schedule for a Minimum line.

## Published minimum-charge clauses (real tariffs)

| Tariff | Service class | Verified language and amounts |
|---|---|---|
| Avista Schedule 1 (WA residential electric) | Residential | Monthly rate: $10.00 Basic Charge plus energy per kWh; **"Minimum Charge: $10.00"** [1] |
| Avista Schedule 11 (WA general service) | General service | $30.00 Basic Charge plus energy and demand; **"Minimum: $30.00 for single phase service and $37.35 for three phase service,"** unless a contract requires more [2] |
| PG&E gas Schedule G-1 (CA) | Residential gas | **Minimum Transportation Charge of $0.13151 per day** — the Transportation Charge "will be no less than" that amount [3] — about $4.08 across a 31-day month (0.13151 × 31) |
| PG&E electric, EM/EM-TOU (CA) | All-electric residential | **Delivery Minimum Bill** of $0.41373 per meter per day, with a CARE discount [4] |

Avista's residential minimum equals its basic charge: at the $10.00 floor with 0 kWh delivered, the bill is $10.00. A 20-kWh month bills $10.00 + (20 × $0.10322) = **$12.06**, clear of the floor [1]. PG&E's G-1 floor scales with the days in the cycle rather than a flat monthly number [3]. Both shapes exist; your tariff decides which one bills you.

## Why a near-zero month still bills

Service costs whether energy flows or not — lines, meter, billing, support. Two mechanisms cover it:

- A flat fixed charge every month — the [customer charge](/blog/17-what-is-customer-charge-on-utility-bill/). On tariffs like Avista Schedule 1, that fixed charge **is** the minimum [1].
- A stated minimum applying when usage charges run thin — the shape of Avista's general-service Minimum and PG&E's per-day minimums [2][3][4].

Many residential schedules carry no separate minimum clause; the always-on fixed charge plays the floor's role — SMUD's residential schedule prices a monthly fixed charge with no minimum-bill clause in the rate table. PG&E went the other way in March 2026: its Base Services Charge "replaces the monthly Minimum Electric Charge" [5], so the floor now appears as the fixed-charge line.

## How to check the floor on your account

1. **Confirm the period**: the cycle's day count and meter status print on the statement; PG&E-style per-day minimums multiply by those days [3][4].
2. **Pull the rate schedule** for your class and look for a line named "Minimum," "Minimum Charge," or "minimum monthly charge" [1][2].
3. **Read its scope** — total bill, delivery-only, or one component. PG&E's minimums are scoped by schedule and customer class, with some groups exempt [3][4].
4. **Recompute the floor**: Avista-style is a flat monthly number; PG&E-style is per-day × days in cycle. Compare with the billed amount.
5. **If billed > floor + usage math**, review the [full line-item breakdown](/blog/electric-bill-breakdown-understanding-line-items/) before disputing — riders, [taxes and fees](/blog/25-utility-bill-taxes-fees-franchise-charges-explained/), or an [estimated read](/blog/14-estimated-utility-bill-explained/) can explain the gap.

For a move-out period, the floor interacts with [proration](/blog/20-utility-bill-proration-explained/) — a short final bill can prorate the fixed charge while the minimum clause still governs the least you can owe.

{{< faq "Is a minimum bill the same as a customer charge?" >}}

They can be equal without being the same thing. The customer charge is a rate component billed monthly; the minimum is a floor on the amount due. On Avista's residential schedule both are $10.00 [1]; on its general service they diverge — $30.00 single-phase minimum, $37.35 three-phase [2].

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Why was my bill the same in a month I was barely home?" >}}

Two published reasons: the fixed charge repeats regardless of use, and some tariffs state a minimum. Avista's residential minimum is $10.00 whatever the usage [1]; PG&E's gas schedule sets a per-day minimum of $0.13151 [3].

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Do minimum charges appear on every bill?" >}}

No — a minimum is a floor, not a fee. It only surfaces in near-zero usage months, and only some schedules state one. Many residential tariffs rely on the always-on fixed charge instead.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Are minimum charges regulated or set by the utility?" >}}

They sit in the utility's filed tariff, approved by the state regulator, and vary by class: Avista states a lower minimum for single-phase, higher for three-phase [2]. Your serving utility's published schedule governs your account.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can solar or a move-out drop my bill to zero?" >}}

Grid-connected solar customers typically still owe the fixed charge — PG&E states its Base Services Charge applies to solar customers as the old minimum electric charge did [5]. On move-out, the final short period is governed by proration and any tariff minimum rather than vanishing.

{{< /faq >}}
