## Quick Answer: What Utilities Cost in Florida {#quick-answer}

**Electricity:** the Florida residential rate averaged **14.99¢/kWh in 2026** (EIA preliminary year-to-date; 15.33¢ final for 2025) — below the 18.16¢/kWh national average. At the EIA national-standard 899 kWh month, that is **$135/month** for electricity.

**Natural gas:** Florida residential gas averaged **$25.48/thousand cubic feet in 2025** (EIA annual) — about **$2.46/therm** after conversion. A standardized 75-therm heating month runs **$184**.

**Water & sewer:** there is **no reliable state average** — water is priced city-by-city in Florida. Nationally the combined bill runs about $70–$90/month; see [what CCF means on a water bill](/blog/what-is-ccf-on-a-water-bill/) to decode yours.

## Why Florida rates look like this {#why-florida-rates-look-like-this}

Florida's electric rate sits below the 18.16¢/kWh national average — on the affordable side nationally. Market structure matters as much as the number: Florida runs regulated, single-utility territories — no retail supplier shopping. That means your rate is set through your utility and the state regulator — the levers that move your bill are usage and rate design (tiers, time-of-use), not supplier shopping. [Which rate plan fits your household](/blog/13-ac-short-cycling-causes-fixes/) sorts the options.

The EIA trend matters too: Florida's residential rate moved from 15.33¢/kWh (2025 final) to 14.99¢/kWh (2026 to date), a 2.2% decrease. On the gas side, residential prices went from $25.37/Mcf in 2024 to $25.48/Mcf in 2025 — a 0% increase. And because the cost driver here is near-continuous air conditioning in a humid climate, your own bill swings more month-to-month than any annual average suggests. For how the pieces fit together, see [how utility rates, fees, and usage work together](/blog/how-utility-rates-fees-usage-work-together/).

## Water and sewer in Florida {#water-and-sewer-in-florida}

Water and sewer are set by your city or water district, not the state — two neighbors across a city line can pay very different rates for the same usage. Instead of a state average, run this check:

1. **Find the unit on your bill** — most water bills measure in CCF (hundred cubic feet). [What CCF means on a water bill](/blog/what-is-ccf-on-a-water-bill/) decodes it in two minutes.
2. **Benchmark usage** — a typical household runs about 2–4 CCF per person per month; well above that, a leak is the usual suspect ([the 10-minute check](/blog/who-responsible-water-leak-before-after-meter/)).
3. **Check fixed charges** — many water systems have raised base charges faster than usage rates; your bill can climb even with flat usage. [Why your water bill is high](/blog/06-water-bill-too-high/) walks the whole statement.

## If your bill runs above these numbers {#if-your-bill-runs-above-these-numbers}

A standardized table tells you what an 899-kWh month costs — not what YOUR month costs. If your electric bill runs well above $135 and your habits haven't changed, the cause is almost always usage, not the rate: near-continuous air conditioning in a humid climate is the usual driver in Florida.

{{< faq "What is the average gas bill in Florida?">}}

Florida residential gas averaged $25.48 per thousand cubic feet in 2025 (EIA) — roughly $2.46 per therm. A standardized 75-therm heating month runs about $184, but gas bills are seasonal: low-use summer months often run $25–$40, while winter peaks far higher. [Why gas bills spike in winter](/blog/07-gas-bill-too-high/) explains the swing.

{{< faq "Is there an average water bill for Florida?">}}

No reliable one — water and sewer rates in Florida are set city-by-city or by water district, so a single state number would be fiction. Compare your bill's CCF usage against the 2–4 CCF per person per month benchmark, and read [why your water bill is high](/blog/06-water-bill-too-high/) if you're above it.
