## Quick Answer

**A quoted energy price is not necessarily the same as the final amount due.** A utility statement can include usage-based charges, customer or service charges, delivery-related charges, taxes, credits, and other tariff-defined items. The only reliable way to understand a charge is to read the description on your bill and the current tariff or rate schedule that applies to your account.

Do not assume a line item is improper because it is unfamiliar, or that a generic online fee range applies to your utility. Ask the utility which tariff provision produced the charge and keep a copy of the answer.

## A Bill Review Process

1. Identify the billing period and total usage.
2. Separate usage-based charges from fixed charges, taxes, credits, and one-time items.
3. Compare the same categories with a similar prior billing period.
4. Read notices, footnotes, and the rate-plan name on the statement.
5. Ask the utility for the tariff, rate schedule, or customer-service explanation for an item you do not understand.

## Questions to Ask About a Charge

| Charge on statement | Useful question |
|---|---|
| Supply or energy | Is this charge based on usage, a fixed amount, or both? |
| Delivery or distribution | Which service and tariff provision does this charge cover? |
| Customer or service charge | Is it recurring, and how is it defined in the tariff? |
| Tax, rider, credit, or adjustment | What rule or program applies to this account? |
| Late fee, deposit, or reconnection item | What policy, deadline, or dispute path applies? |

## If You Cannot Pay a Bill

Contact the utility promptly and ask about payment arrangements, assistance programs, and any available protections. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau directs consumers seeking year-round utility-bill help to their local LIHEAP office or the National Energy Assistance Referral Hotline.[2]
