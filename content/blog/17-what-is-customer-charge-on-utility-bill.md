## Quick Answer

**Open your bill, find the flat monthly line for metering, billing, and customer service, then check its amount against your utility's published rates — that line is the customer charge, and it does not shrink when you use less.** A customer charge is a fixed fee billed once per month per meter to cover the cost of keeping your account service-ready. Your utility may call it a Customer Charge, Basic Service Charge, System Access fee, or System Infrastructure Fixed Charge — the label varies; the mechanics do not [1][3][4][5].

Decision rule: if the amount changes with your kWh or therms, it is not the customer charge. If it repeats at the same amount on statements that disagree on usage, it is.

## Verified customer charges (published rates, not ranges)

| Utility | Charge name | Verified amount | Where published |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMUD (Sacramento, CA) | System Infrastructure Fixed Charge | $27.00/month per meter as of Jan. 1, 2026 (scheduled $27.80 on Jan. 1, 2027; $17.00/month on the Low Use rate) | Residential Time-of-Day rate schedule [1] |
| Austin Energy (Austin, TX) | Residential Customer Charge | $16.50/month, inside City of Austin | Residential rates and line items [2] |
| PG&E (northern CA) | Base Services Charge | around $24.00/month standard; around $6.00 (CARE) and $12.00 (FERA) low-income, March 2026 restructure | Base Services Charge FAQ [3] |
| Consumers Energy (Michigan) | System Access (electric) | $8.00/month fixed fee | Electric charges explained [5] |

SMUD describes its fixed charge as paying for "poles, wires, transformers, meter equipment, billing and customer service" [1]. Con Edison defines its Basic Service Charge (or Customer Charge) as "a flat, monthly fee" covering meter readings, billing, and customer support [4]. Consumers Energy's gas Customer Charge is "a fixed monthly fee" for the same cost family [6]. Same structure, different name and number — which is why the tariff, not the label, is the authority.

On Austin Energy's sample 860-kWh residential bill, the $16.50 customer charge is one line among tiered energy charges, a regulatory charge, community benefit and power supply adjustments, and city tax — itemized lines totaling $116.45 [2]. Keep that context: the customer charge is real, named, and small next to the usage-driven lines.

## How to check yours

1. **Record the exact label, amount, and service class** from your statement — customer-charge names differ by utility [1][3][5].
2. **Find the utility's published rate for it.** Most utilities post customer-charge amounts on their rates pages [2][3][5].
3. **Read what the utility says it covers.** SMUD, Con Edison, and Consumers Energy all state the covered costs in plain terms [1][4][6].
4. **Separate it from everything else**: usage charges, [riders and surcharges](/blog/19-utility-bill-surcharges-and-riders-explained/), [taxes and franchise fees](/blog/25-utility-bill-taxes-fees-franchise-charges-explained/), and one-time fees.
5. **Watch for scheduled changes.** SMUD publishes its increases in advance ($26.20 → $27.00 → $27.80 across effective dates) [1], and PG&E's March 2026 restructure folded its old minimum electric charge into the Base Services Charge [3].

## It is not these other charges

- **One-time fees**: a [connection fee](/blog/27-utility-connection-fee-explained/) attaches to starting service and a [reconnection fee](/blog/28-utility-reconnection-fee-explained/) to restoring it — the customer charge repeats monthly.
- **Usage-linked recovery**: a [fuel adjustment charge](/blog/18-fuel-adjustment-charge-on-utility-bill-explained/) moves with fuel costs; the customer charge is flat.
- **Delivery vs supply**: the customer charge sits on the delivery side of the [electric bill line-item breakdown](/blog/electric-bill-breakdown-understanding-line-items/).

{{< faq "Why do I pay a customer charge in months I barely used anything?" >}}

Because it prices fixed service costs, not consumption: metering, billing, customer support, and grid readiness [1][4]. It holds whether usage is 0 or 2,000 kWh.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is the customer charge the same as a minimum bill?" >}}

No. The customer charge is an always-on cost component; a [minimum bill](/blog/22-minimum-bill-utility-bill-explained/) is a floor on the total amount due. On some tariffs the two share the same number by design — the distinction is the function.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Do all utilities charge the same amount?" >}}

No. Among rates published this month by four utilities, fixed charges run from $8.00/month (Consumers Energy System Access) to $27.00/month (SMUD), with PG&E's around $24.00 and Austin Energy's $16.50 [1][2][3][5]. Check the serving utility's own rate page.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can the customer charge change?" >}}

Yes. Utilities adjust it through scheduled rate changes — SMUD's schedule steps up through 2027 [1] — and partial-period bills may prorate it; see [how proration works](/blog/20-utility-bill-proration-explained/).

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can I get it waived?" >}}

The base amount is set in a published schedule, not negotiated case by case. What exists are program discounts: PG&E's CARE and FERA fixed-charge discounts [3], and SMUD's low-income and medical-equipment discounts on the bill [1]. Ask the utility which assistance programs apply to your account.

{{< /faq >}}
