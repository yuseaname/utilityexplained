## Quick Answer

**A customer charge is a rate component that must be interpreted from the serving utility's tariff, rate schedule, and bill.** DOE rate-design material describes residential rates as historically including a fixed customer charge and a volumetric rate, but the amount, label, purpose, and applicability are utility-specific.[1]

Do not assume the charge is the same for all accounts, always applies at zero usage, has a national average amount, or can be compared to another provider without reviewing the applicable tariff.

## Find the Applicable Tariff

Record the charge as it appears on the statement, along with the service class, rate-schedule name, billing period, and effective date. Then find the tariff section that defines:

- whether the charge is fixed, usage-related, or part of another calculation;
- which account class it applies to;
- how often it is billed;
- what other charges are separate; and
- which policy governs a change, correction, or dispute.

## Compare the Full Rate Structure

DOE notes that utility bills can have several cost components that are determined differently, and changes in usage can affect those components differently.[2] Compare the customer charge with usage charges, demand charges if applicable, riders, taxes, credits, and one-time items rather than treating it as the explanation for the entire bill.

When the bill changes, compare a similar prior statement for billing days, rate-plan changes, and tariff notices.

## Questions for the Provider

1. Which tariff section defines this customer charge for my account?
2. What service class and rate schedule apply?
3. Is any part of this charge dependent on use, billing days, or another account condition?
4. Which charges are separate from it?
5. What review or dispute process applies if it appears incorrect?

Keep the bill, tariff version, and provider response together. Do not stop payment or assume a waiver is available without confirming the provider's current written policy.
