## Quick Answer

**Do not choose a portable space heater from a generic “best” list or expect a universal utility-bill saving.** First confirm that portable heat is appropriate for the room, that the home's electrical setup and manufacturer instructions support it, and that it can be operated safely. DOE identifies safety as a top consideration for small space heaters.[1]

Use the exact wattage, operating time, and your own electric tariff to understand the added electricity use; do not rely on a national hourly-cost estimate.

## Start With Electrical and Fire Safety

Follow the manufacturer instructions for the exact heater. CPSC’s portable electric heater safety alert says to keep the heater at least three feet away from beds, sofas, curtains, and other items that can burn.[2]

CPSC also advises plugging electric space heaters directly into a wall outlet—not an extension cord or power strip—and turning them off when sleeping.[3] If an outlet, circuit, cord, or breaker behaves unexpectedly, stop using the device and consult a qualified electrician.

## Determine Whether Portable Heat Fits the Room

Before buying or operating a heater, document:

| Question | What to check |
|---|---|
| Room and occupants | Size, use, ventilation, sleeping areas, children, pets, and mobility needs |
| Electrical setup | Outlet condition, circuit use, and manufacturer electrical requirements |
| Heat need | Existing heating performance, drafts, and whether a building/HVAC issue needs repair |
| Equipment | Exact model, manufacturer manual, controls, shutdown features, and recall status |
| Utility impact | Rated wattage, expected operating time, billing-period rate, and other bill charges |

Portable heaters are not a substitute for correcting unsafe electrical conditions, combustion hazards, or a failed primary heating system.

## Calculate Your Account-Specific Use

The heater’s manual or label identifies its rated electrical demand. Record that figure, expected hours of use, and your utility's current bill or tariff. Then compare the expected use with your household's existing bill and rate structure. Delivery charges, time-based rates, taxes, and other components may affect the total amount due.

Do not assume a lower setting, timer, technology label, or product category guarantees lower total heating cost or suitable comfort. Compare the whole home and the actual usage pattern.

## Questions Before Purchase or Use

1. Does this exact model’s manual permit the intended room and use?
2. Can it be plugged directly into a suitable wall outlet with clear space around it?
3. Are there recall, maintenance, or manufacturer-warning notices for the model?
4. Is the existing central or primary heating system functioning safely?
5. What would the rated use add under my current tariff and billing rules?
