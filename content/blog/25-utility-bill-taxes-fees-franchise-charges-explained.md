## Quick Answer

**Tax, fee, franchise, rider, credit, and other bill lines must be identified from the account’s own statement and controlling documents.** Their names, amounts, legal basis, calculation method, and ability to change vary by provider, service territory, rate plan, and local rules. Do not apply a national percentage or assume that a similarly named charge has the same meaning elsewhere.

DOE electricity-bill guidance describes multiple bill charge types and rate structures, so a line-by-line review is more reliable than treating the amount due as one undifferentiated charge.[1]

## Make a Bill-Line Inventory

For each non-usage line, record its exact name, amount, rate basis, and document reference.

| Charge type | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Tax or government charge | Jurisdiction, rate, tax base, effective date, and official reference |
| Franchise or local charge | Provider description, local agreement or rule reference, and calculation |
| Rider or surcharge | Tariff section, eligible service class, rate, and adjustment period |
| Public-program or policy charge | Program name, administrator, authority, and account applicability |
| Credit or adjustment | Reason, period covered, and how the amount was calculated |

Separate these lines from usage, supply, delivery, demand, fixed, and one-time charges. The same bill can include several different calculation methods.

## Compare the Right Documents

Compare the bill with the current tariff, rate schedule, provider bill guide, and—where relevant—the local or state source cited by the provider. Check the billing period, service class, effective dates, credit status, and calculation basis before concluding a charge increased or is incorrect.

DOE notes that utility rate options and bill components can be determined differently, and changes in use can affect them differently.[2]

## Questions for the Provider

1. What tariff, policy, or government reference defines this line item?
2. What rate, base, and effective period produced the amount on my bill?
3. Is the charge usage-based, fixed, percentage-based, credited, or one-time?
4. Which service class and account conditions make it applicable?
5. What review process applies if the bill line appears incorrect?

For tax, legal, or deduction questions, use the relevant tax authority or a qualified professional rather than relying on a generic utility-bill article.
