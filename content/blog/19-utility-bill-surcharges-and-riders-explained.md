## Quick Answer

**A surcharge or rider is an account-specific bill component whose meaning, amount, purpose, and effective dates must be confirmed in the current tariff and bill.** DOE electricity-bill guidance explains that bills can be reproduced using the applicable rate structure and measured energy use and demand.[1]

Do not assume a rider is temporary, regulatory, universal, non-negotiable, or associated with a particular program from its name alone. Similar labels can have different calculations and applicability across providers.

## Create a Rider Inventory

Record every rider or surcharge exactly as it appears on the bill.

| Detail | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Label | Exact bill wording and tariff reference |
| Applicability | Service class, rate plan, account conditions, and effective dates |
| Calculation | Fixed, usage-based, percentage-based, demand-based, credit, or other basis |
| Scope | What the tariff says the item covers |
| Duration | Start/end, adjustment, review, or replacement terms in the tariff |
| Related lines | Charges, credits, taxes, and one-time items that are separate |

Keep the tariff version with the bill. A bill may contain charges with similar names that have different bases or periods.

## Verify the Calculation

Compare the billed rate and quantity with the applicable tariff and service period. Check meter/read status, billing days, usage, demand if applicable, rate-plan version, credits, and other line items before attributing a total-bill change to one rider.

DOE notes that utility rate options and bill components can be determined differently and can be affected differently by changes in use.[2] If an item does not reconcile, request the account-specific calculation from the provider.

## Questions for the Provider

1. Which current tariff section defines this rider or surcharge?
2. Why does it apply to my service class and account during this period?
3. What rate, quantity, and effective dates produced the billed amount?
4. Is the item fixed, usage-based, demand-based, percentage-based, credited, or otherwise calculated?
5. What stated review or adjustment schedule applies?

Do not assume a provider program, rebate, complaint path, or opt-out applies to a rider without confirming its current written terms.
