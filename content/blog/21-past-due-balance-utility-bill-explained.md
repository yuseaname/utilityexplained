## Quick Answer

**A past-due balance is the amount a utility says you owe from earlier bill periods, and the date printed on a disconnection notice — not the balance itself — is the deadline that matters.** Reconcile the account, verify the notice, line up assistance, and get a payment agreement before that date.

Help exists year-round: LIHEAP assists roughly 6.7 million households with heating and cooling bills [2], and the CFPB tells customers to contact a local LIHEAP office or call the National Energy Assistance Referral Hotline at (866) 674-6327 [1].

## Step 1: Reconcile the Account First

Compare the current statement with the prior statement and your payment records — [how utility billing cycles work](/blog/23-utility-billing-cycle-explained/) explains why the two statements can cover different day counts. Record:

| Detail | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Past-due amount | The exact figure and which bill periods it covers |
| Current charges | Latest service period, separate from the past-due line |
| Payments and credits | Date, amount, confirmation number, and how the utility applied them |
| Fees | Late charges or reconnection items — ask which term authorizes each |
| Notice | The stated due date, disconnection date, and contact instructions |

If a payment doesn't match your own records, the next move is [how to dispute a utility bill](/blog/how-to-dispute-utility-bill/) — open the review before you pay anything you can't verify. In Texas, a utility cannot disconnect you for disputed charges until the dispute is resolved and you are notified of the outcome [5].

## Step 2: Verify the Notice and the Deadline

Every state sets its own notice framework — the pattern is notice, then a waiting period, then disconnection:

| Framework | Verified rule |
|---|---|
| Minnesota Cold Weather Rule (public utilities) | No disconnection of primary heat October 1–April 30 for households at or below 50% of state median income who pay on a payment agreement; disconnection needs 10 working days' notice by mail (7 if personally served), and never on a Friday, weekend, holiday, or day before a holiday [3] |
| Minnesota Cold Weather Rule (municipal and cooperative utilities) | Same October 1–April 30 window; disconnection is barred until at least 30 days after a mailed notice or 15 days after personal delivery [4] |
| Texas (electric) | Disconnection requires a 10-day notice period after a written disconnection notice is issued; the date cannot fall on a weekend or holiday [5] |

Your state's public utility commission publishes the equivalent consumer rules; read them against your notice rather than assuming any other state's timeline. For the whole arc — notice types, waiting periods, and what utilities may and may not disconnect for — see [can a utility shut off your service?](/blog/can-utility-shut-off-service/).

## Step 3: Line Up Assistance Before the Deadline

CFPB directs customers to year-round utility-bill help through the local Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) office, or the National Energy Assistance Referral Hotline at (866) 674-6327; for help finding options, contact 211 [1]. LIHEAP runs through state block grants paid to local agencies, and the funds are targeted specifically to prevent shutoffs and assist with heating and cooling bills [2]. For the full landscape of programs — LIHEAP and beyond — see [utility assistance programs: LIHEAP and other help for bill payments](/blog/utility-assistance-programs-liheap/).

Confirm with the program:

- **Eligibility** — what income or household rules apply to you.
- **Deadlines** — whether the program can act before your disconnection date.
- **Required documents** — what to gather before applying.
- **Payment routing** — whether payment goes to you or directly to the utility.

In Texas, if your REP receives notice by the final due date on the disconnection notice that an energy assistance provider is forwarding sufficient payment — and you've handled any balance the assistance doesn't cover — your service cannot be disconnected for non-payment [5].

## Step 4: Dispute and Protect Service at the Same Time

Ask for a payment agreement before the disconnection date, not after. Texas customers who have received no more than two termination notices in the past year are eligible for a deferred payment plan [6]. Under Minnesota's Cold Weather Rule, a utility also may not charge a new deposit or delinquency charge to keep service when you've entered a payment agreement (or appealed a payment-agreement dispute to the commission) [3].

If a specific line is wrong, open a review using the [utility-bill dispute process](/blog/how-to-dispute-utility-bill/) while keeping the payment agreement — disputing a charge does not put the rest of the bill on hold. Know the downside before you decide: [utility reconnection fee: what to expect after shutoff](/blog/28-utility-reconnection-fee-explained/) covers the cost side if a shutoff does happen.

## Keep a Written Record

Keep bills, notices, payment confirmations, and case numbers together. Ask for the next review level and the oversight authority if the utility can't resolve the question — investor-owned, municipal, and cooperative utilities answer to different regulators, and Minnesota customers can appeal a payment-agreement dispute directly to the state commission, which must decide within 20 working days [3]. As you build the file, [how to read your electric bill line by line](/blog/01-how-to-read-your-electric-bill/) is the reference for decoding the statement behind the balance.

A disconnection notice is recoverable: [reconnection after shutoff](/blog/28-utility-reconnection-fee-explained/) explains the fee side, and [the full shutoff-and-notice lifecycle](/blog/can-utility-shut-off-service/) explains when utilities can disconnect at all. Deposit demands often arrive with a new past-due situation — how to [review a utility deposit](/blog/26-utility-deposit-explained/).

**Where to go next:** Work the four steps above against your own notice today — reconcile the account, verify the deadline, call LIHEAP or your utility for a payment agreement before that date, and keep every confirmation in one file. If the balance itself doesn't match your records, hand the statement to [how to dispute a utility bill](/blog/how-to-dispute-utility-bill/) and keep the payment agreement running while the review is open.

## Frequently Asked Questions

{{< faq "How long before the utility shuts off service after a past-due balance?" >}}

Long enough for a written notice with a 10-day clock — that's the verified pattern in Texas [5] and under Minnesota's Cold Weather Rule framework, where mailed notices require 10 working days (30 calendar days for Minnesota municipal and cooperative utilities) [3][4]. Read the dates on your own notice; they control your deadline.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Who can help me pay a past-due utility balance?" >}}

Your local LIHEAP office — CFPB lists it as the year-round entry point, with the National Energy Assistance Referral Hotline at (866) 674-6327 [1]. LIHEAP serves roughly 6.7 million households through local agencies [2]. For anything else, 211 can help you identify what's available.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Can I dispute a past-due amount without losing service?" >}}

In Texas, no disconnection for disputed charges until the dispute is decided [5]. In Minnesota, appealing a payment-agreement dispute to the commission bars disconnection while the appeal is pending, with a 20-working-day decision clock [3]. Elsewhere, ask the utility in writing what protection applies while your review is open.

{{< /faq >}}
