## Quick Answer

**A sewer charge pays for wastewater service, but the calculation and labels vary by utility.** Your statement and the applicable local rate schedule are the reliable sources for determining whether the charge is fixed, usage-based, capped, adjusted, or calculated another way.[1]

Do not assume a winter-average method, irrigation exception, or universal sewer-to-water price relationship applies to your account. Read the line-item description, billing period, units, and tariff before drawing a conclusion.

## Start With the Statement

EPA WaterSense notes that utilities use different units and rate structures, and that many water bills combine fixed and volume-based charges.[1] Sewer service may be shown on the same statement as water service or issued separately, depending on the provider.

For each bill, record:

1. The billing period and account address.
2. The water-use unit shown on the statement, if any.
3. The exact sewer line-item name and amount.
4. The current rate-plan or tariff name.
5. Any notices, credits, adjustments, or footnotes.

## Questions to Ask the Utility

Ask customer service for the current rate schedule and these account-specific answers:

- Is this sewer charge fixed, usage-based, or a combination?
- If it uses water consumption, which billing period and unit are used?
- Is there a minimum charge, maximum billable use, seasonal rule, or separate irrigation-meter policy?
- Does the account have a credit, adjustment, or past-due item?
- Where can I read the tariff section that defines this charge?

Keep the reply and the version date of the rate schedule with your bill records.

## Compare Like With Like

Compare the same line items with a similar earlier billing period. Check the number of billed days and whether the water-use unit, rate plan, or meter-read status changed. A higher total alone does not identify the cause.

If the statement reports water use and the change is unexplained, use a no-use meter check and inspect for leaks. EPA WaterSense offers household maintenance guidance, while the utility can explain the billing rule that applies after a leak is found.[1]

## When a Charge Looks Wrong

Document the bill, current meter reading if relevant, and the date you contacted the provider. Ask whether the statement used an estimated read and whether the provider can explain or review the specific line item. Do not withhold payment or assume an adjustment is available without confirming the provider's dispute and payment policies.
