## Quick Answer

**Read an electricity bill by separating account details, billing period, meter and usage information, rate schedule, and itemized charges.** The names, charge categories, meter displays, and supplier options vary by provider, so the serving utility's bill guide and tariff control the interpretation.

Con Edison, for example, presents supply and delivery charges on an itemized bill, with related charges and taxes identified in its sample-bill materials.[1][2]

## Start With the Billing Period

Record the service address, account class, rate-plan name, billing-period dates, and number of billed days. Compare a similar period before treating a change in the total amount due as a change in household use.

## Review Meter and Usage Information

Check the current and prior readings, unit, and whether the statement identifies an actual, estimated, or other read status. The difference between readings must be interpreted under the provider's billing rules, including any multiplier or conversion shown on the bill.

Con Edison publishes instructions for reading and submitting both digital and dial-meter readings for its customers; follow the instructions of your own serving utility and never open or alter a meter.[3]

## Separate the Itemized Charges

List the categories exactly as they appear on the statement. They may include usage-related charges, supply charges, delivery charges, fixed charges, taxes, credits, or other tariff-defined items. Do not assume a national supply-to-delivery percentage, a charge name, or a provider-choice rule applies to the account.

If an item is unclear, request the current tariff or bill guide and ask which rate-schedule section defines it.

## Compare Like With Like

When the total changes, compare:

1. Billing days and read status.
2. Total kWh and any interval data.
3. Rate-plan and tariff changes.
4. Fixed charges, taxes, credits, and one-time items.
5. Major changes in weather, occupancy, equipment, or charging.

The total bill alone cannot show whether the change came from kWh use, price, fees, or a billing adjustment.

## When to Contact the Utility

Contact the provider if a reading, rate schedule, or charge does not match the statement or the current tariff. Keep the bill, any meter documentation, the tariff version, and a record of the provider's response.
