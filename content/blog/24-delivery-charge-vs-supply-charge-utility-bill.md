## Quick Answer

**“Delivery” and “supply” are bill categories whose definitions, rates, subcharges, and provider options depend on the serving utility and the account's tariff.** Do not assume one category is fixed, that a particular percentage applies, or that a customer can choose a different provider without checking the account's current terms.

Con Edison describes supply and delivery charges on its bills and explains that delivery costs are based on energy use; use the equivalent guide and tariff from your own provider to interpret your account.[1]

## Read the Itemized Statement

Record each line as it appears on the bill:

| Bill detail | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Supply | Rate, usage basis, provider name, and related charges |
| Delivery | Rate, usage basis, fixed components, and related charges |
| Taxes, riders, and credits | Whether the item is separate from supply or delivery |
| Rate plan | Tariff name, service class, and effective date |
| Meter information | Usage unit, billing days, and actual or estimated status |

Some providers present additional supply or delivery components. Con Edison's Market Supply Charge information, for example, describes a per-kWh market supply charge along with other supply charges.[2]

## Compare the Full Bill

When comparing offers or prior bills, use the same scope each time. A supply-only advertised price is not the same as the total amount due. Compare billing days, usage, delivery components, supply components, taxes, credits, and one-time items before deciding that one category caused a change.

## Questions for the Provider

1. Which tariff sections define my supply and delivery charges?
2. Which parts are usage-based, fixed, seasonal, or time-variable?
3. Does this account have a supplier choice or a provider-specific offer?
4. What current rate and contract documents apply to this account?
5. Which charges changed from the prior bill, and why?

Keep the bill, tariff version, offer terms, and provider response together when reviewing a charge.
