## Quick Answer

**A water meter only moves when water is flowing through it.** If the dial or [leak indicator](/blog/water-meter-leak-indicator-explained/) is still spinning with every faucet, appliance, and sprinkler zone off, water is escaping somewhere — and you have a leak.

The question that decides everything: **which side of the meter is it on?** One five-minute test — shutting your main water valve and watching the meter — tells you whether the fix is yours or the water utility's.

## The Five-Minute Main-Valve Test

1. **Make sure nothing uses water.** No dishwasher, no ice maker filling, no sprinkler timer about to start. Flush nothing for 30 minutes before the test.
2. **Watch the meter.** Confirm the sweep hand or small leak indicator is still creeping.
3. **Shut the main shutoff valve** — usually where the water pipe enters your house, or next to the meter itself.
4. **Watch the meter for five more minutes.**
   - **Meter stopped** → water was flowing *inside your plumbing*. The leak is on your side. Work through the list below.
   - **Meter still spinning** → water is moving *before* your valve — between the meter and the street. That's the utility's service line. Call them.

Write down which happened. If you later call a plumber or the utility, that one observation saves a diagnostic fee.

## On Your Side: The Four Usual Suspects

**1. A running toilet — the most common by far.** A warped flapper silently drains the tank all day, and a [running toilet can waste 200+ gallons per day](/blog/is-your-toilet-running-leak-test/). Drop a little food coloring in the tank; if color reaches the bowl without flushing, you found it. A $15 flapper kit usually fixes it.

**2. A stuck irrigation valve.** A sprinkler zone valve that won't fully close leaks underground where you'll never see it. If the meter creeps mostly at night, check the controller's log — or shut the irrigation backflow valve and see if the meter stops.

**3. The water heater's T&P relief valve.** Temperature-and-pressure relief valves that fail open trickle constantly — often into a drain pan you never inspect. Run your hand along the discharge tube; warmth means it's releasing.

**4. A hidden pipe or slab leak.** The bad one. Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water inside walls, or unexplained dampness point here. This is plumber territory — or a leak-detection service if nothing else checks out.

## On the Utility's Side: The Service Line

If the meter spins even with your main valve closed, the water is escaping **between the meter and the water main — pipe that belongs to the water utility, not you.** Don't dig, and don't pay a plumber to guess: call the utility's emergency line and report a suspected service-line leak. The repair is typically their responsibility and their cost.

One caveat: in some jurisdictions, the homeowner owns the portion of the service line on their property. Ask the utility to confirm where the responsibility line falls before authorizing any work.

## What a Spinning Meter Costs You

Leaks don't just raise the water line of your bill. Because most utilities base [sewer charges on your water usage](/blog/how-sewer-charges-work-on-your-water-bill/), every leaked gallon can be billed twice — once as water, once as sewer. A moderate continuous leak can push [your bill dramatically higher](/blog/why-did-my-water-bill-suddenly-increase/) without a single extra drop reaching your taps, and a serious one can waste **up to 36,000 gallons a month**.

If the leak turns out to be on the utility's side, ask for a billing adjustment for the period the service line was leaking. Many utilities will credit the usage-based portion once they confirm their repair.
