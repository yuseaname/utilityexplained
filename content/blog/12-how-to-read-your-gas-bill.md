## Quick Answer

**A gas bill must be interpreted from the account's meter information, billing period, usage unit, rate schedule, and itemized charges—not from a generic national price, therm conversion, or seasonal bill table.** DOE materials describe regulated and deregulated gas-market structures, but the controlling terms for a household are the serving utility's current tariff and bill.[1]

Keep the statement and tariff together when reviewing a charge or comparing periods.

## Start With the Account Details

Record these items before comparing a bill:

1. Service address, account class, and rate-schedule name.
2. Billing-period start and end dates and total billed days.
3. Current and prior meter readings, read status, and unit shown.
4. Total billed usage and any conversion factor shown by the utility.
5. Each itemized charge, credit, tax, rider, or one-time item.

Utilities can bill gas use in different units and may apply account-specific conversion or rate rules. Use the unit and calculation shown in the provider's own materials rather than assuming CCF, MCF, and therms are interchangeable in every circumstance.

## Separate Usage From Other Charges

Group the bill into the categories actually shown on the statement. These may include usage-related charges, fixed charges, delivery-related charges, taxes, credits, or other tariff-defined items. Do not assume the label, amount, or purpose matches another utility's bill.

Ask the utility for the exact tariff section that defines an unfamiliar charge. DOE's utility-bill guidance is useful context for understanding market structures, but it does not replace the local tariff.[1]

## Compare Like With Like

When a total changes, compare a similar billing period and record:

- the number of billing days;
- billed gas use and read status;
- weather and occupancy changes;
- rate-schedule or tariff changes; and
- credits, past-due amounts, or one-time items.

The total amount due alone cannot identify whether a change came from usage, a rate, or a non-usage charge.

## When to Contact the Utility

Contact the provider if a meter reading, usage unit, rate schedule, or line item does not match the statement or current tariff. Ask for the account-specific explanation, the tariff version, and the provider's review or dispute process. Keep copies of the bill and your contact record.
