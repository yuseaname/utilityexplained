## Why gadgets work: the three utility money leaks

**Leak 1: Invisible electricity.** You pay for every kilowatt-hour but see none of them. The average US home wastes $100–$200/year on standby power alone. You cannot cut what you cannot see — which is why the first purchase should always be a meter, not a fix.

**Leak 2: Silent water.** Water leaks are the scariest utility line item because the bill arrives a month after the damage. A leaking toilet wastes up to 200 gallons a day. A dripping faucet, 3,000 gallons a year. Your meter's leak indicator tells you THAT something leaks — but only a sensor network tells you WHERE.

**Leak 3: Dumb schedules.** Heating and cooling run when nobody's home and sleep when rates peak. A thermostat with a schedule you never program is a thermostat without a schedule.

## What we deliberately left out

- **Smart showerheads and faucet aerators with apps** — the hardware saves, the app is theater. A $5 aerator does the same job.
- **Power factor correction devices** — residential versions are scams. Your home's power factor is already fine; your utility doesn't bill residential PF anyway.
- **Magnetic water conditioners** — no measurable effect on hardness or bills. Skip.
- **"Energy saver" plug-in boxes** — 100% scam category. If a $40 box could cut bills 40%, utilities would give them away.

If a gadget's savings claim can't be expressed in watts, gallons, or runtime hours, it isn't a savings claim. It's marketing.

## How we ran the numbers

Payback math assumes the EIA national averages for 2026: electricity at roughly **$0.17/kWh**, natural gas around **$15/thousand cubic feet**, and water/sewer at a combined **$11–$15 per thousand gallons** in most metros.
