## Quick Answer

**A bill covering less or more than a usual period must be reviewed using its stated service dates, meter/read status, tariff, and account records.** Do not assume every provider prorates fixed charges by day, uses the same move-in/out rule, treats real-estate closings alike, or calculates usage from a generic monthly formula.

Con Edison’s sample residential bill identifies service dates, meter information, usage, rate, and charges—fields that should be reviewed on any partial-period statement.[1]

## Gather the Right Records

For a new, final, transferred, or rate-change bill, collect the provider’s confirmation of the service start/end date, the statement, applicable tariff, and payment or account correspondence. If the property changed hands, keep the closing, lease, or occupancy documentation, but confirm directly with the provider which account date controls its billing.

| Record | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Service confirmation | Start/end date, service address, and account holder |
| Statement | Service period, billing days, rate plan, and itemized lines |
| Meter information | Prior/current reading, unit, and actual or estimated status |
| Tariff | Fixed, usage, minimum, credit, and other partial-period rules |
| Account correspondence | Provider explanation, case reference, or final-bill terms |

## Compare the Bill With the Account Dates

Check whether the billed service period begins and ends on the account dates documented by the provider. Then separate usage-based lines from fixed, minimum, tax, credit, and one-time lines. Each may follow a different tariff calculation, so a partial billing period does not prove every line must be reduced in the same proportion.

If the bill uses an estimated read, follow the provider’s documented review process. Con Edison explains that account holders can submit a meter reading in certain circumstances to avoid an estimated bill; use your provider’s instructions because requirements vary.[2]

## Questions for the Provider

1. What service dates, rate plan, and meter/read status were used for this statement?
2. Which tariff sections govern a partial-period bill for this account?
3. How were usage, fixed, minimum, credit, tax, and one-time lines calculated?
4. Is any reading estimated, corrected, or customer-submitted?
5. What review process applies if the service dates or calculation do not match the account record?

Do not alter a meter or equipment. For a move, closing, or final-account question, obtain the provider’s written instructions before relying on a title-company, landlord, seller, or tenant assumption.
