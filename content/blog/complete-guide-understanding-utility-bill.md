## Quick Answer

**A useful bill review starts with your own account records, not national average costs or a generic line-item table.** Identify the service period, meter-read status, billed usage, rate plan, each charge, credits, taxes, and any one-time items. Then compare the same fields with an earlier statement and the current tariff.

DOE explains that electricity bills can range from detailed statements to shorter summaries depending on the utility company, and its bill guides cover consumption, demand, riders, and rate structures.[1][2][3]

## Gather the Documents

Use the current bill, a comparable prior bill, your account’s rate schedule or tariff, and the provider’s bill guide. For a question about payment history, also use the account ledger or payment confirmation—not memory alone.

| Document | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Current statement | Current service dates, amount due, meter information, and charges |
| Prior comparable statement | What changed in usage, rates, credits, or billing days |
| Tariff/rate schedule | How the account's price and charge rules are defined |
| Provider bill guide | The provider’s labels, units, and account procedures |
| Account history | Payments, adjustments, correspondence, and open cases |

## Step 1: Establish the Billing Scope

Record the service-period start and end dates, total billing days, statement date, and due date. Then record the service type (electricity, gas, water, sewer, or another service), account number, rate-plan name, and meter-read status.

Do not compare a total amount due from one bill directly with another until you know whether the two bills cover similar dates and the same rate schedule. A changed period, credit, payment status, or one-time charge can affect the total without identifying the reason for a usage or rate change.

## Step 2: Verify Meter and Usage Information

Locate the prior and current meter reading or other usage record, the billed unit, and the provider’s read status. Electricity may use kWh; gas and water providers may use other units, and the account’s bill should show the unit and any provider-specific conversion or calculation.

Compare the billed usage with the prior statement only after checking billing days and meter status. If the reading is estimated, customer-submitted, corrected, or unclear, use the provider’s documented process to request an explanation or review.

## Step 3: Inventory Every Charge

Make a separate list of each charge rather than grouping everything into “the bill.”

| Category | What to document |
|---|---|
| Usage or consumption | Quantity, unit, rate, rate period, and calculation |
| Supply/delivery or similar categories | Exact provider labels and tariff references |
| Fixed, demand, tiered, or time-based items | Account applicability, unit, threshold, and timing rule |
| Riders, taxes, fees, and public charges | Rate, base, effective date, and official/tariff reference |
| Credits and adjustments | Reason, billing period, and calculation |
| One-time items | Description, date, and provider explanation |

DOE's electricity guide identifies consumption, demand, and riders as distinct bill components, and its natural-gas guide discusses common charges and rate structures.[1][2]

## Step 4: Compare the Current Bill With a Prior One

Create a small comparison table for the fields that matter:

| Field | Current bill | Prior comparable bill | Question to answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Billing days |  |  | Did the service period change? |
| Meter/read status |  |  | Is one reading estimated or corrected? |
| Billed usage |  |  | Did the quantity or unit change? |
| Rate plan/tariff |  |  | Did the effective rate schedule change? |
| Each itemized charge |  |  | Which exact component changed? |
| Credits/one-time items |  |  | Is the total affected by an adjustment? |

This approach produces an evidence trail for a provider call and prevents a vague “my bill is too high” dispute.

## Step 5: Ask the Provider Specific Questions

Ask for the tariff section and account-specific calculation, not a general explanation. Useful questions include:

1. Which tariff and service class apply to this account for this billing period?
2. How was billed usage calculated from the meter or account data?
3. Is the reading actual, estimated, corrected, or customer-submitted?
4. Which exact line items changed from the prior comparable bill, and why?
5. What credits, riders, taxes, or one-time charges are included?
6. What documented review process applies if a calculation appears incorrect?

Keep copies of the bills, tariff version, screenshots, payment confirmations, case number, and provider response together.

## When to Escalate Safely

Contact the provider promptly if there is an unexplained meter status, payment discrepancy, duplicate-looking item, incorrect service date, or rate-plan question. Follow the provider’s written billing-review process. Do not alter meters, utility equipment, or account records, and do not assume a charge is wrong until the provider or applicable authority confirms the calculation.
