## Quick Answer

**Treat unexpected cool air from a heating system as a safety question first, not a DIY repair challenge.** Work through this check order before you call anyone:

1. **Thermostat mode** — is it set to HEAT and the set point above the room temperature?
2. **Fan setting** — is the fan on AUTO, not ON?
3. **Air filter** — is it clean, or is it clogged and blocking airflow?
4. **Breaker and service switch** — is the furnace getting power?
5. **Exterior intake and exhaust** — are they clear of snow, ice, or debris?
6. **Carbon-monoxide alarm** — does the test button work?

If your carbon-monoxide alarm sounds, you smell fuel or smoke, or anyone has symptoms such as headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, or confusion, stop here and get help immediately.[1]

Do not bypass safety controls, open combustion equipment, relight a pilot, clean ignition parts, or diagnose a heat exchanger from an online article. Those steps require the equipment manual and, when appropriate, a qualified heating professional.

## 15-Minute Check Table

| Check | What to look for | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Thermostat mode | Set to HEAT, set point above room temp | Set it to HEAT and raise the set point 5° above current room temperature; wait 30 seconds |
| Fan setting | FAN on AUTO, not ON | Flip the fan to AUTO — this is the number-one cause of cold air blasts |
| Air filter | Hold it up to light — can you see light through it? | Replace a 1-inch filter or clean a washable one; a clogged filter can trip the limit switch on modern furnaces |
| Breaker + service switch | Breaker on, service switch in ON position | Reset the breaker once, then leave the room; if it trips again, call a pro |
| Exterior intake/exhaust | Clear of snow, ice, lint, or nesting debris | Clear the obstruction — the pipe is the furnace's breathing tube |
| CO alarm test | Press and hold the test button | If it doesn't sound, replace the batteries; if it still fails, replace the alarm |

If all these pass and the air is still cold after 5 minutes, move to the cause list below.

## Cause List: DIY vs. Call a Pro

### 1. Thermostat set to FAN ON (or COOL)
- **DIY:** Switch the fan to AUTO and set the system to HEAT. This fixes the vast majority of calls.
- **Call a pro?** Rarely needed.

### 2. Clogged filter → limit switch shutdown
- **DIY:** Replace the filter and reset the breaker if needed. On many modern furnaces, a clogged filter causes the burners to shut off while the blower keeps running, pushing out cold air.
- **Call a pro?** Only if a new filter and breaker reset do not restore heat within 5 minutes.

### 3. Condensate drain clog (90%+ condensing furnaces)
- **DIY:** If your furnace has a plastic drain line near the PVC vent, check for a clog and clear it per the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Call a pro?** If the drain is inaccessible or the furnace still locks out.

### 4. Pilot or ignition fault
- **DIY:** On older standing-pilot units, you may relight the pilot per the manual. On modern units with hot-surface igniters, this is not a DIY job.
- **Call a pro?** Most igniter repairs require a technician.

### 5. Cracked heat exchanger
- **DIY:** No — stop.
- **Call a pro?** Yes. A cracked heat exchanger can allow carbon monoxide into your home. Turn the furnace off and call a qualified professional immediately. See the safety block below.

## When to Stop and Get Help

Leave the home and call emergency services or the appropriate emergency contact if a carbon-monoxide alarm sounds or a person may have carbon-monoxide poisoning. The CPSC advises moving to fresh air and seeking immediate medical attention in that situation.[1]

Contact a qualified heating professional if the system continues to move cool air, repeatedly shuts down, has an unusual odor or sound, leaks water or fuel, or displays a fault message you cannot resolve through the owner's manual.

## Cost of Repairs

Costs vary by region — get three quotes from licensed technicians before approving any repair. We do not list specific dollar figures here because local labor and parts prices differ widely, and there is no reliable national benchmark that fits every home.

**What to expect when you call:**
- A diagnostic visit typically comes with a flat fee that is credited toward the repair if you use that contractor.
- Simple fixes like a filter change or thermostat setting are often free if you do them yourself.
- Igniter or flame sensor replacements usually take less than an hour.
- Blower motor replacements are larger jobs and can vary significantly by unit type.

The best approach is to ask for itemized quotes and compare at least three. Avoid contractors who push a full system replacement without showing you a failed part.

## Replacement Reality

If your furnace is more than **15–20 years old**, you may be entering repair-versus-replace territory. Modern condensing furnaces are significantly more efficient than older units, and the U.S. Department of Energy's Energy Saver site notes that furnace efficiency is measured by AFUE (Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency). Typical furnace lifespans run **15–20 years** with proper maintenance.[^energy]

**Important:** The federal 25C tax credit for high-efficiency furnaces expired for property **placed in service after 12/31/2025** — the 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA) ended it early; the IRS confirms the credit applies only through December 31, 2025 [3]. It has not been renewed as of 2026-08-30. Do not let anyone tell you a federal rebate is still available for a new furnace — it is not. Your state or utility may offer separate incentives, so check the DSIRE database if you are considering a replacement.

A properly sized, well-maintained furnace can last **15–20 years**; after that, a major repair may cost more than a partial replacement over the same period. Get three quotes for both repair and replacement before deciding.

## FAQ

{{< faq "Why does cold air blast out when the fan runs?" >}}
Usually the thermostat fan is set to **ON** instead of **AUTO**. In AUTO mode, the blower runs only when the furnace is actually heating. Flipping it to AUTO often solves the problem immediately.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How often should I change the filter in winter?" >}}
With daily heating, replace a 1-inch fiberglass filter every **1 to 3 months**. If you have pets or allergies, lean toward the shorter end of that range. A clean filter protects the blower and helps avoid limit-switch shutdowns.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "Is a little cold air at startup normal?" >}}
Yes. It is normal to feel a short burst of cool air for the first **1–2 minutes** while the burner warms up and the blower starts. If the air stays cold after 5 minutes, use the check table above.
{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "When should I stop troubleshooting and call someone?" >}}
If you smell gas or burning odor, see soot, hear a loud bang, or your carbon-monoxide alarm sounds — stop right now and call a professional (or 911 for a gas emergency). Otherwise, if one pass through the 15-minute table does not restore heat, a service call is the next step.
{{< /faq >}}

## Preventing Avoidable Problems

Use the maintenance schedule in the equipment manual, keep required vents clear, and use carbon-monoxide alarms as recommended by CPSC.
