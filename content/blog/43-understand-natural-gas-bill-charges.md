## Quick Answer

**Natural-gas bill categories and calculations vary by provider, tariff, contract, and account.** Review the statement’s own meter information, billing days, usage units, rates, credits, taxes, and additional line items rather than applying a generic supply/delivery split or a national fee range.

DOE’s natural-gas bill guidance notes that bills depend on pricing and contract structure and that some charges appear every month while others do not.[1]

## Record the Bill Details

Use the current statement and a comparable earlier statement to record:

| Detail | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Billing period | Start/end dates and total days |
| Meter information | Meter number, prior/current reading, and actual or estimated status |
| Usage unit | Unit shown, conversion factor if any, and billed quantity |
| Rate plan | Tariff, service class, and effective date |
| Charges | Usage, fixed, supply, delivery, tax, credit, rider, and one-time items |

The terminology may differ between providers. Use the applicable tariff and the provider's bill guide as the controlling explanation for the account.

## Compare Like With Like

Compare bills with similar billing days and seasonal conditions where possible. Separate a change in the billed quantity from a change in the rate, read status, fixed charge, credit, or one-time item. An increase in the total amount due does not identify a single cause by itself.

DOE’s natural-gas bill resource is intended to help readers analyze gas bills and classify or categorize charges.[2] Use it as background guidance, then obtain the provider-specific calculation for your account.

## Ask the Provider Targeted Questions

1. Which tariff sections define each charge on my current bill?
2. Is my meter reading actual, estimated, corrected, or otherwise adjusted?
3. How was the billed usage quantity calculated from the meter information?
4. Which rate, rider, tax, credit, or one-time item changed from the prior statement?
5. Can you provide the account-specific calculation and current tariff version?

For a gas odor, suspected leak, or other emergency, follow the serving utility’s emergency instructions rather than attempting to diagnose or repair gas equipment.
