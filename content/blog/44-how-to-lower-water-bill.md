## Quick Answer

**A high water bill should be investigated from the statement, meter, property conditions, and local utility rules before assuming a particular fixture, product, or habit will lower the cost.** EPA WaterSense provides home-maintenance and leak-detection resources, while the serving utility controls the billing unit, rate structure, and any adjustment policy.[1][2]

Avoid generic dollar-savings promises or product rankings. Start with the account's actual usage and billing period.

## Compare the Bill First

Put the current bill beside a comparable earlier statement and record:

1. Billing days and meter-read status.
2. Total use and the unit shown by the utility.
3. Fixed, usage-based, tax, credit, and one-time charges.
4. Household, irrigation, shared-service, or occupancy changes.
5. Current rate schedule and any provider notices.

Compare daily use if the billing period differs. A higher total does not by itself identify a leak or a billing error.

## Check for Water Flow When None Is Intended

Follow the meter and safety instructions from your local utility. During a period when water should not be in use, observe the meter or account data and document any unexpected change. EPA WaterSense's resources can help guide leak detection and maintenance, but plumbing access and repair decisions should match the property and equipment involved.[1]

For rental housing or shared service, notify the landlord or property manager in writing and ask how water use and repairs are allocated.

## Choose Changes From Evidence

After identifying a pattern, prioritize the verified cause: a repair, fixture maintenance, irrigation setting, occupancy change, or a provider question. Confirm local utility rules before expecting a bill adjustment, rebate, or savings result.

WaterSense provides information on water-saving products and maintenance, but product selection should use the installed fixture, property conditions, and local program terms—not a generic payback claim.[1]

## When to Contact the Utility or a Professional

Contact the utility if the reading, billing unit, rate schedule, or line item is unclear. Contact a qualified plumbing or irrigation professional for suspected hidden leaks, damage, or work that is not safe to perform. Keep the bill, meter observations, repair records, and provider response together.
