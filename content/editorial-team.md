Utility Explained is produced by a single independent researcher focused on translating utility bills into plain English; the "desks" below are topic areas, not separate staff. Guides may use AI assistance during drafting. That assistance is not evidence: data-heavy pages are being reviewed so material figures and regulatory claims can carry visible, dated links to primary sources.

## Our Desks

Bylines on our guides identify the topic desk responsible for a guide. They are not claims of independently credentialed individuals. Each desk owns its topic area end to end — research, drafting, and review.

### Bills & Rates Desk {#bills-rates-desk}

The Bills & Rates Desk owns our electricity, gas, water, and billing-structure guides — decoding every line item and rate plan on your bill. It breaks down how electricity, gas, and water bills are put together: what each line item means, how rate structures (flat, tiered, time-of-use, demand charges) change what you pay, and where billing errors and estimated reads tend to hide.

For data-heavy billing guides, the desk uses primary sources such as U.S. EIA data, state public utility commission filings, and published provider tariffs. Refreshed guides show their source links and review date. A representative desk piece is [The complete guide to understanding your utility bill](/blog/complete-guide-understanding-utility-bill/); its rate and usage figures come from the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Electric Power Monthly (eia.gov), cited with retrieval dates.

### Home Energy Desk {#home-energy-desk}

The Home Energy Desk owns our energy-efficiency and savings guides — showing you how to lower your bills, what each fix costs, and how long it takes to pay for itself. It covers lowering household utility use, from weatherstripping and water-heater settings to HVAC efficiency ratings (SEER, AFUE, HSPF, COP), heat pumps, and air sealing.

For data-heavy efficiency guides, the desk uses DOE, ENERGY STAR, and manufacturer documentation. Refreshed guides show their source links and review date. A representative desk piece is [DIY home energy audit](/blog/47-check-energy-leaks-home-diy/), which walks the DOE leak checklist room by room; the desk anchors on the U.S. Department of Energy's Energy Saver guides (energy.gov/energysaver), citing the pages it uses with retrieval dates.

### Affordability & Assistance Desk {#affordability-assistance-desk}

The Affordability & Assistance Desk owns our budgeting, assistance-program, and cost-planning guides — helping you plan for utility expenses and access help when you need it. It covers budgeting for utilities, level-pay plans, assistance programs, and avoiding shutoffs.

Budget benchmarks use the U.S. Energy Information Administration's state-average price series and CFPB payment-help guidance, cited with retrieval dates. A representative desk piece is [How to budget for utilities in your first apartment](/blog/how-to-budget-for-utilities-in-your-first-apartment/).

### Heating & Cooling Desk {#heating-cooling-desk}

The Heating & Cooling Desk owns our heating and cooling guides — explaining how your HVAC system drives your utility bills and what to do when something seems off. It covers efficiency ratings, heat-pump operation, duct leaks, short cycling, and common HVAC problems behind unexplained bill spikes.

For data-heavy HVAC guides, the desk uses manufacturer documentation and DOE material. Refreshed guides show their source links and review date. A representative desk piece is [Why is my furnace blowing cold air](/blog/why-is-my-furnace-blowing-cold-air/); safety guidance in desk guides follows the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's carbon-monoxide materials (cpsc.gov), cited with retrieval dates.

### Consumer Rights Desk {#consumer-rights-desk}

The Consumer Rights Desk owns our fees, troubleshooting, and consumer-rights guides — helping you read a bill critically and challenge charges that look wrong. It covers fees, riders, surcharges, billing disputes, and state public-utility-commission complaint processes, explaining which charges are regulated and pointing to the real dispute and complaint channels available to consumers.

A representative desk piece is [How to dispute a utility bill](/blog/how-to-dispute-utility-bill/); dispute and shutoff-protection guidance draws on state utility-consumer-protection rules and the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's utility-help resources (consumerfinance.gov), cited with retrieval dates.

## Our Editorial Process

Every article follows a defined process:

1. **Research** — For a data-heavy guide, we collect the relevant primary sources: EIA data, published utility tariffs, state regulations, or program-owner guidance.
2. **Draft** — Guides may use AI assistance to produce a clear first version efficiently.
3. **Verify** — Before a data-heavy guide is published or refreshed, its material claims must be checked against the linked source material.
4. **Publish or hold** — A guide with unverified, time-sensitive, or unsupported claims is held for revision rather than presented as a current reference.

We are transparent that AI assistance is part of our workflow. Readers can inspect the source links and review date on a refreshed guide; older pages are under review. See our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) and [methodology](/methodology/) for details.
