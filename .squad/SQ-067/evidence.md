## 2. COMPARISON TABLES (all arithmetic shown)

### Assumptions (label these on the page)

- Electricity 18.34¢/kWh (R1) — national average, "preliminary"; reader swaps in own all-in rate.
- Gas $1.56/therm = six-month avg $16.23/Mcf ÷ 10.37 (R2, R3); sensitivity at $1.35 and $1.91/therm.
- Space heater 1,500 W = 5,118 Btu/h output (R4), COP 1 by physics (P1).
- Gas furnace 80,000 Btu/h input, 80% AFUE → 64,000 Btu/h delivered (P8 for the 95–97% ENERGY STAR alternative; 80% = standard-efficiency assumption).
- Electric furnace 20 kW (four 5-kW elements; P6) → 68,240 Btu/h (20,000 × 3.412).
- Heat pump: DOE's live "≈65% electricity reduction vs resistance" ≈ seasonal COP 2.86 (1 ÷ 0.35). Legacy DOE "50%" ≈ COP 2.0. We print the live 65% (P3).
- Duty cycles are illustrative (thermostat cycling), stated per row — not measured data.

### Table A — Full-fire cost per hour (equipment running flat out)

| System | Arithmetic | $/hour (at $1.56/therm) |
|---|---|---|
| Space heater, 1,500 W | 1.5 kW × $0.1834 | **$0.28** |
| Gas furnace, 80k Btu/h input | 0.8 therm/h × $1.56 | **$1.25** (range $1.08 @ $1.35/therm – $1.53 @ $1.91) |
| Electric furnace, 20 kW | 20 kW × $0.1834 | **$3.67** |
| Heat pump delivering 68,240 Btu/h | 20,000 W ÷ 2.86 = 6,993 W × $0.1834 | **$1.28** |

Caveat to print: furnaces cycle; nobody pays full-fire × 24 h. This table sizes burners, not bills.

### Table B — Cost per 10,000 Btu DELIVERED (the apples-to-apples table)

| System | Arithmetic | per 10,000 Btu |
|---|---|---|
| Any resistance electric (space heater or electric furnace) | 10,000 ÷ 3,412 = 2.93 kWh × $0.1834 | **$0.54** |
| Gas @ 80% AFUE, $1.56/therm | 10,000 ÷ 0.80 = 0.125 therm × $1.56 | **$0.195** (at $1.35: $0.169; at $1.91: $0.239) |
| Gas @ 95% AFUE, $1.56/therm | 10,526 Btu in = 0.1053 therm × $1.56 | **$0.164** |
| Heat pump, COP 2.86 | 10,000 ÷ (2.86 × 3.412) = 1.025 kWh × $0.1834 | **$0.188** |
| Gas with 25% duct loss, 80% AFUE | $0.195 ÷ 0.75 | **$0.26** |

Derived break-evens (print as "at national averages"):
- Gas is **2.2–3.2× cheaper per Btu** than resistance electric (2.75× at $1.56/therm).
- Gas would have to exceed **$4.30/therm** (0.8 × $5.375 per therm-equivalent delivered) before a space heater matched it per Btu — no mainland state average is close.
- Electricity would have to fall below **≈6.7¢/kWh** to match $1.56/therm gas ($1.56 ÷ 23.45 kWh-equivalent per therm at 80% AFUE).

### Table C — The task that matches the search query: one room, one winter evening (8 h, Nov–Mar ≈ 150 days)

Assumption: one occupied room ≈ 8% of a whole-home design load (5,118 of 64,000 Btu/h); heater thermostat cycles it ~50%.

| Approach | Evening arithmetic | Per evening | Per season (150) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space heater, ~50% duty | 1.5 kW × 8 h × 0.5 = 6 kWh × $0.1834 | **$1.10** | **$165** |
| Space heater, full duty (upper bound, matches page 42) | 12 kWh × $0.1834 | $2.20 | $330 |
| Central gas furnace heating the WHOLE house those 8 h (~40% burner duty) | 0.8 therm/h × 8 h × 0.4 = 2.56 therms × $1.56 | $3.99 | ~$599 |
| Central electric furnace, whole house (~40% duty) | 20 kW × 8 h × 0.4 = 64 kWh × $0.1834 | $11.74 | ~$1,761 |
| Central heat pump, whole house (~40% duty) | 6.99 kW × 8 h × 0.4 = 22.4 kWh × $0.1834 | $4.10 | ~$615 |

Reading (honest): the heater's evening wins on price because it heats ~1/12th the volume — even though its heat costs ~2.75× more per Btu (Table B). The $3.99 gas evening bought whole-house warmth, which is a different product.

### Table D — Whole-home replacement (why "space heaters instead of central heat" fails)

Whole home needing 64,000 Btu/h on a cold day:

| Approach | Arithmetic | $/hour |
|---|---|---|
| Gas furnace | 0.8 therm/h × $1.56 | $1.25 |
| Electric furnace | 64,000 ÷ 3,412 = 18.76 kW × $0.1834 | $3.44 |
| Heat pump (COP 2.86) | 64,000 ÷ (2.86 × 3,412) = 6.56 kW × $0.1834 | $1.20 |
| Space heaters | 64,000 ÷ 5,118 = **12.5 heaters** = 18.75 kW × $0.1834 | $3.44 |

Plus the electrical reality: 12.5 heaters × 12.5 A ≈ 156 A at 120 V — each 15-A circuit carries one heater at ~83% capacity (S2 direct-outlet rule). Whole-home heating with plug-in heaters is a physics and fire-service non-starter, not just a budget one.

### Break-even room share (derived, label as estimate)

Space heater beats central gas once the heated share of the home is below roughly 1 ÷ 2.75 ≈ **36%** at $1.56/therm — wider (≈45–50%) if the duct system leaks 20–30% as ENERGY STAR says is typical (P7), tighter at $1.35/therm or with a 95%+ AFUE furnace. Versus central **electric resistance**, the space heater wins at any share < 100% (same fuel, same efficiency; zoning is the only lever — DOE's >20% zone-heating claim, P2). Versus a **heat pump**, resistance space heating costs ~2.9× per Btu; the heater wins only for short single-room spot needs.

## 3. CAVEATS (the honesty spine of the page)

1. **Space heaters cannot heat a whole home** — Table D; also circuit-load reality (S2).
2. **"Cheaper" is true only for single-room use with the rest of the home set back.** The DOE zone-heating framing (P2) requires occupied-rooms-only heating with unoccupied areas left cooler — and even DOE notes the cool zones still need heat to protect plumbing (archived page's qualifier).
3. **Setback interaction:** DOE's 7–10°F / 8-h / up-to-10%-a-year rule (P5) is the mechanism that makes the strategy work: heater in the occupied room + setback on the central thermostat.
4. **Safety asymmetry:** space heaters are ~30% of home-heating fires but 73% of heating-fire deaths (S1); CPSC usage rules apply (S2). Electric units produce no CO (S3) — the CO risk is fuel-burning equipment.
5. **Per-Btu honesty:** if the reader's alternative is resistance electric (electric furnace/baseboard), a space heater saves nothing per unit of heat — savings come only from heating less area. If it's gas or a heat pump, the heater's energy is 2.2–3.2× / ~2.9× costlier per Btu.
6. **Rates vary more than the averages:** Northeast electricity ~24¢/kWh vs West ~20¢ (R5); gas ranged $1.35–$1.91/therm across six months (R2). The page's per-tariff formula is the durable answer.
