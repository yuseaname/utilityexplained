## A2 — do-thermal-curtains-really-work
- DOE factsheet (energy.gov PDF, 2021): "Installing window attachments can save 15% of a household's annual HVAC" energy use (cellular shades vs vinyl blinds study context). https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-12/bto-cellular-shades-factsheet-112221.pdf (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- AERC (Attachments Energy Rating Council): independent rating/certification of window attachments (U-factor, solar heat gain, air leakage); cellular shades 5-16% savings potential in certified-product materials. https://aercenergyrating.org/available-products/ (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- The "reduces window heat loss 25-40%" figures circulating on retailer/brand pages = MANUFACTURER-REPORTED, source-of-study murky — the article's job is to separate these tiers (DOE/AERC measured vs marketing).
- Mechanism physics (safe to explain, no citation needed as it's textbook): still-air layer + edge-of-window convection blocking; benefit scales with window quality (single-pane > modern double-pane) and FIT (overlap past frame, close to wall).
- NICETOWN/Deconovo = Amazon top-selling thermal-curtain brands (Best Seller badges in insulated thermal curtains category; e.g. NICETOWN B077RPXSF3 seen live 2026-08-30). Typical band $20-50/panel-pair.
- Summer mechanism: blocking solar gain through glass (same hung barrier, daytime operation).
- Operation discipline: closed at night in winter / closed on sun-hit windows in summer; open on sunny winter days for free solar gain.

## A3 — what-merv-rating-do-i-need
- EPA "What is a MERV rating?": MERVs report filter capture ability for particles 0.3-10 µm; higher = finer capture. https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/what-merv-rating (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- CDC/EPA guidance referenced for MERV 13+ where fine-particle capture matters — with the airflow caveat. https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/what-merv-rating
- American Standard (manufacturer guidance): residential typically MERV 8-13; higher MERV can restrict airflow. https://www.americanstandardair.com/resources/glossary/merv/ (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- FilterBuy: most homes use MERV 8/11/13. https://filterbuy.com/resources/air-filter-basics/which-merv-rating-should-I-use/ (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- Wirecutter pick context (for positioning only, do not clone): Filtrete MPR 1900 (MERV 13 equivalent) "highest most systems can handle."
- Mechanism law: a clogged filter makes the blower work against restriction (ties to site art. 13 ac-short-cycling); replacement cadence 1-3 months typical; SIZE is the #1 return reason (measure L×W×D).
- CAVEAT LAW: never tell readers to blind-upgrade to MERV 13 — check manual/system; high static pressure risk is real.

## A5 — how-to-find-and-seal-drafts-in-your-home
- ENERGY STAR Seal and Insulate: air sealing + insulation "can provide up to a 10% savings on your total annual energy bill" (program-stated, label as ENERGY STAR estimate). https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate (retrieved 2026-08-30)

- energy.gov: insulation + air-sealing tax-credit context (25C Home Improvement Credit covers insulation/air sealing). https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/articles/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit-insulation-and-air-sealing (retrieved 2026-08-30)
- Product field (tracer-confirmed live 2026-08-30): rope caulk $8-15 (press-in) · adhesive V-strip/D-profile $5-15 · under-door sweeps Holikme B07MSM6G9R $8-15 · weighted fabric stoppers $15-25 (zero-install) · outlet gasket 50-packs $5-15 (breaker off, screwdriver) · attic-hatch insulation covers $25-60 · chimney flue plugs $12-60 (MUST remove before any fire).
- 10-spot tour (DOE/FTC-standard leak locations): attic hatch, rim joist/foundation, outlets+switches (exterior walls), window sashes, door bottoms/sweeps, baseboards/floor edges, fireplace damper/flue, dryer vent, pipe/duct penetrations, wall/ceiling penetrations (fans, vents).

- Detection: hand test, incense/smoke pencil, night flashlight test — NOT the thermal camera (art. 47 owns that).
