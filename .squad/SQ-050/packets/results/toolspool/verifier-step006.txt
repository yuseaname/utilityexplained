The only reliable way to understand a charge is to read the description on your bill, the current tariff or rate schedule that applies to your account, and the local or state rule that authorizes the charge.</p><p>This page is the launching pad for the site&rsquo;s fee-explainers. Use the table below to identify what a charge is, then follow the linked explainer for the full review process, the questions to ask your provider, and the documents to keep.</p><h2 id=a-bill-review-process>A Bill Review Process</h2><ol><li>Identify the billing period and total usage.</li><li>Separate usage-based charges from fixed charges, taxes, riders, credits, and one-time items.</li><li>Compare the same categories with a similar prior billing period.</li><li>Read notices, footnotes, and the rate-plan name on the statement.</li><li>Ask the utility for the tariff, rate schedule, or customer-service explanation for an item you do not understand.</li></ol><h2 id=the-charges-you-may-see>The Charges You May See</h2><p>The table below lists seven categories of charges that appear across U.S. residential electricity, gas, and water bills. Each row links to the site&rsquo;s full explainer for that charge. Amounts and structures vary by provider, state, service class, and rate plan — the table summarizes what published utility sources describe (DOE rate-design material, Con Edison&rsquo;s bill guide, and CFPB consumer guidance) and tells you what to look for on your own statement.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Charge</th><th>What it is</th><th>How it typically appears</th><th>How to spot it on your bill</th><th>Read more</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Customer charge</strong> (a.k.a. basic service charge, service charge, monthly minimum)</td><td>A fixed monthly amount the utility bills for being connected to the system, regardless of how much energy or water you used. DOE rate-design material describes residential rates as historically built around a fixed customer charge plus a volumetric rate; the amount, label, and applicability are set in the utility&rsquo;s tariff.<sup id=fnref:1><a href=#fn:1 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>1</a></sup></td><td>Flat dollar amount billed every cycle, even on near-zero usage months.</td><td>A line that does not vary with kWh, therms, CCF, or gallons — and is on the bill whether or not you used the service.</td><td><a href=/blog/17-what-is-customer-charge-on-utility-bill/>Customer charge explainer</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Transmission / delivery rider</strong> (a.k.a. transmission surcharge, distribution charge, delivery service charge)</td><td>A usage-based charge that covers the wires, pipes, substations, and the people who maintain them. Con Edison describes delivery as the part of the bill that funds the infrastructure; &ldquo;customers who use more pay more of these shared costs.&rdquo;<sup id=fnref:2><a href=#fn:2 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>2</a></sup></td><td>A cents-per-kWh (or per-therm, per-CCF) line multiplied by your usage. May be split into &ldquo;transmission&rdquo; and &ldquo;distribution&rdquo; sub-rows on restructured bills.</td><td>Look for &ldquo;delivery,&rdquo; &ldquo;distribution,&rdquo; &ldquo;transmission,&rdquo; &ldquo;grid charge,&rdquo; &ldquo;wires charge,&rdquo; or your utility&rsquo;s equivalent. Multiply the rate by your usage and confirm against the line total.</td><td><a href=/blog/24-delivery-charge-vs-supply-charge-utility-bill/>Delivery vs supply charges</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Fuel / energy adjustment charge</strong> (a.k.a. fuel cost recovery, energy cost adjustment, purchased-gas adjustment)</td><td>A usage-based charge that passes through the provider&rsquo;s wholesale fuel or energy cost for the period. DOE electricity-bill material notes that consumption charges can appear under different names and are billed at a rate determined by a contract.<sup id=fnref:3><a href=#fn:3 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>3</a></sup></td><td>Cents-per-kWh (or per-therm) line that changes month-to-month as the underlying fuel or wholesale energy price changes.</td><td>Look for &ldquo;fuel adjustment,&rdquo; &ldquo;fuel cost recovery,&rdquo; &ldquo;energy cost adjustment,&rdquo; &ldquo;purchased gas adjustment,&rdquo; or &ldquo;wholesale supply.&rdquo; Compare this month&rsquo;s rate with last month&rsquo;s — a jump is normal; a structural change is not.</td><td><a href=/blog/18-fuel-adjustment-charge-on-utility-bill-explained/>Fuel adjustment explainer</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Surcharges, riders, and policy riders</strong> (environmental, renewable portfolio, energy efficiency, public benefits, system benefits charge)</td><td>Account-specific add-ons whose meaning, amount, purpose, and effective dates are defined in the tariff. DOE electricity-bill material notes that bills can be reproduced using the applicable rate structure and measured energy use; riders are one component in that structure.<sup id=fnref1:3><a href=#fn:3 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>3</a></sup></td><td>Varies — can be a flat per-bill amount, a cents-per-kWh rate, or a percentage of another charge.</td><td>Look for &ldquo;rider,&rdquo; &ldquo;surcharge,&rdquo; &ldquo;environmental,&rdquo; &ldquo;renewable,&rdquo; &ldquo;energy efficiency,&rdquo; &ldquo;public benefits,&rdquo; or &ldquo;policy adjustment&rdquo; — and read the tariff section that defines each one.</td><td><a href=/blog/19-utility-bill-surcharges-and-riders-explained/>Surcharges and riders explainer</a> · <a href=/blog/25-utility-bill-taxes-fees-franchise-charges-explained/>Taxes, fees, and franchise charges</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Taxes and franchise / local-government charges</strong></td><td>Federal, state, and local taxes on utility service; franchise fees paid to a city or county for the right to operate in that jurisdiction; gross-receipts taxes. Their names, rates, and legal basis vary by jurisdiction.<sup id=fnref:4><a href=#fn:4 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>4</a></sup></td><td>Either a percentage of another line (subtotal, delivery, or full bill) or a flat per-bill amount, depending on the local rule. May be lumped into a single &ldquo;taxes&rdquo; line or split into &ldquo;state tax,&rdquo; &ldquo;city tax,&rdquo; &ldquo;franchise fee,&rdquo; &ldquo;public utility tax.&rdquo;</td><td>Identify the jurisdiction by ZIP code or service address; ask the utility which tax authority each line pays. Con Edison documents that local property taxes on energy infrastructure total more than $3.2 billion for customers in 2026, and notes that most of this is embedded in delivery rather than listed separately.<sup id=fnref1:2><a href=#fn:2 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>2</a></sup></td><td><a href=/blog/25-utility-bill-taxes-fees-franchise-charges-explained/>Taxes, fees, and franchise charges</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Minimum bill / minimum charge</strong></td><td>A floor below which the bill will not fall, even at zero usage. May be the same as the customer charge, or it may be a higher floor that also includes some usage.<sup id=fnref1:1><a href=#fn:1 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>1</a></sup></td><td>When your usage × rate would compute to less than the minimum, the bill shows the minimum instead.</td><td>Look for &ldquo;minimum charge,&rdquo; &ldquo;minimum bill,&rdquo; &ldquo;monthly floor,&rdquo; or any line that does not move down with usage. Compare with the <a href=/blog/17-what-is-customer-charge-on-utility-bill/>customer charge explainer</a> to determine whether the two are the same item on your account.</td><td><a href=/blog/22-minimum-bill-utility-bill-explained/>Minimum bill explainer</a></td></tr><tr><td><strong>One-time / event-driven fees</strong> (late fee, deposit, reconnection, service-start / connection)</td><td>Charges tied to a specific account event — paying past the due date, opening an account, restoring service after disconnection, or starting service at a new address. The amount, refundability, and conditions are defined in the provider&rsquo;s tariff and account terms, not by a national rule.<sup id=fnref:5><a href=#fn:5 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>5</a></sup></td><td>Usually a single line item with a one-time dollar amount. May appear separately from monthly charges or be grouped under &ldquo;account activity,&rdquo; &ldquo;other charges,&rdquo; or &ldquo;adjustments.&rdquo;</td><td>Look for &ldquo;late fee,&rdquo; &ldquo;delinquent charge,&rdquo; &ldquo;deposit,&rdquo; &ldquo;connection fee,&rdquo; &ldquo;service start,&rdquo; &ldquo;reconnection,&rdquo; or &ldquo;restoration.&rdquo; Confirm each in writing against the provider&rsquo;s tariff before paying.</td><td><a href=/blog/27-utility-connection-fee-explained/>Service-start / connection</a> · <a href=/blog/28-utility-reconnection-fee-explained/>Reconnection fee</a> · <a href=/blog/21-past-due-balance-utility-bill-explained/>Past-due balance</a></td></tr></tbody></table><h2 id=why-this-page-does-not-give-you-a-national-price-range>Why This Page Does Not Give You a National Price Range</h2><p>Every fee page on this site repeats the same caveat: the amount, label, calculation, and applicability of any charge depend on the <strong>serving provider&rsquo;s tariff, the service class on your account, and the state or local rules that apply</strong>.<sup id=fnref2:1><a href=#fn:1 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>1</a></sup><sup id=fnref2:3><a href=#fn:3 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>3</a></sup><sup id=fnref2:2><a href=#fn:2 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>2</a></sup><sup id=fnref1:4><a href=#fn:4 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>4</a></sup><sup id=fnref1:5><a href=#fn:5 class=footnote-ref role=doc-noteref>5</a></sup> Two customers on different utilities — or even two customers on different rate plans at the same utility — can see the same charge name at very different amounts. A &ldquo;customer charge&rdquo; of $8 in one territory and $24 in another is normal, not an error; what looks like a wrong number may be the right number for the wrong reason, or the wrong number under the wrong rule.</p><p>This is also why the table above describes the <strong>structure</strong> of each charge (flat dollar, per-kWh, percentage of subtotal, one-time event) rather than a dollar range. Confirm the amount on <strong>your</strong> bill against <strong>your</strong> utility&rsquo;s current tariff — not against an article on the internet.</p><h2 id=how-to-use-the-linked-explainers>How to Use the Linked Explainers</h2><p>For each charge you do not understand, the linked page gives you the same workflow used across this site:</p><ol><li>Record the line as printed on the statement, including the tariff or rate-schedule reference.</li><li>Find the tariff, rate schedule, or local rule that defines the charge.</li><li>Confirm the calculation: fixed, usage-based, demand-based, percentage, credit, or one-time.</li><li>Compare with a similar prior billing period to isolate what changed.</li><li>If the line does not reconcile, request the account-specific calculation from the provider before assuming the charge is wrong.</li></ol><p>The full directory of fee and bill-charge explainers lives below;
