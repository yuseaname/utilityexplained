Water Meter Running When No Water Is Being Used: Causes and Fixes

Margaret Harrington May 27, 2026 6 min read

Quick Answer

If your water meter keeps spinning or clicking when every faucet and appliance is off, water is flowing somewhere — and that means a leak. The three most common culprits, in order:

  1. A silent running toilet — a worn flapper valve lets water trickle into the bowl 24 hours a day. This is the #1 cause, and it’s a $15 fix.
  2. A stuck irrigation valve — a sprinkler zone that never fully closes leaks underground where you’ll never see it.
  3. A service-line or slab leak — the pipe between the street meter and your house can crack underground, wasting up to 36,000 gallons a month.

The 10-minute test below tells you which one you have — and whether the leak is inside your house or under your yard.

The 10-Minute Test: Find Where the Leak Is

Step 1: Kill every drop of water use

  • No faucets, showers, or hose bibs dripping
  • Dishwasher and washing machine off (not mid-cycle)
  • Ice maker off — unplug it if you have to
  • Sprinklers off, including “smart” schedules

Wait 5 minutes so toilets finish refilling after their last flush. A refilling toilet looks exactly like a leak.

Step 2: Confirm the meter is really moving

Watch the meter face for 60–120 seconds. On analog meters, watch the sweep hand or the small red triangle leak indicator (it moves with even a trickle — see our leak indicator guide for what your meter’s version looks like). On digital meters, watch the flow icon or gallons-per-minute readout.

Not moving? You don’t have a continuous leak right now. Take a photo of the reading, wait 15 minutes with everything still off, and compare — tiny leaks can be too slow for the indicator but still show on the numbers.

Step 3: Shut the main valve — the deciding move

Close the main water valve where the pipe enters your house (usually in the basement, crawlspace, or by the foundation). Watch the meter again.

  • Meter stopped? The leak is INSIDE your house. Go to Step 4.
  • Meter still running? The leak is BETWEEN the meter and your house — that’s your service line or, if it surfaces at your foundation, a slab leak. Skip to Step 5.

If It’s Inside: Find the Culprit

Test the toilets first (the usual suspect)

Put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank. Don’t flush. Wait 20–30 minutes. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking — replace it ($10–15 at any hardware store, 10-minute job). Test every toilet; the quiet bathroom you never use is a favorite hiding spot.

A running toilet wastes 200–400 gallons a day — that’s $50–100 a month on most water bills.

Then check:

  • Water heater — look at the T&P relief valve discharge pipe: dripping means a failing valve
  • Under sinks — feel the supply lines and cabinet floors for moisture
  • Washing machine hoses — bulges, cracks, or damp floor behind the machine
  • Refrigerator line — dampness under or behind the fridge where the icemaker line runs

Drop a water leak sensor under the water heater, sinks, and toilets — they’ll catch the next one at $25 instead of at the meter.

If It’s Outside: Service Line and Slab Leaks

A meter that keeps running with the house valve closed means water is escaping between the meter box and your foundation. Signs to look for:

  • Unusually green, soggy, or sunken patches in the yard between the meter and house
  • Warm spots on the floor (hot-line slab leaks)
  • Cracks in the foundation or doors that suddenly stick
  • The sound of running water where nothing is on — listen at the foundation at night

This is plumber territory. Service-line leaks don’t fix themselves, and excavation or slab repair costs grow with every week you wait. Call your water utility too — some will credit part of a leak if you report it and document the repair.

Whose Problem Is It? The Meter Draws the Line

Responsibility divides at the water meter:

  • Utility side (street to meter): the water company’s problem, their cost. If the meter itself is running with the house valve closed AND their side is leaking, report it — some utilities will even check for free.
  • Your side (meter to house and everything inside): your problem, your plumber.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a water meter run without a leak? Rarely. If you’ve verified everything is truly off — including ice makers and smart irrigation — a moving meter means flow. The one exception: some utilities briefly cycle or flush lines nearby, which can nudge the indicator for a few minutes. If the meter moves continuously for 10+ minutes with the house valve shut, treat it as a leak.

How do I know if it’s the utility’s side or mine? The main-valve test in Step 3 answers this. Meter stops with the valve closed = your side. Still running = between the meter and valve.

Will my water utility charge me for a leak on my side? Yes — you’re billed for everything that passes through the meter. Many utilities offer a one-time leak adjustment or credit if you report the leak and prove it was fixed. Ask before you pay the bill.

How much does a plumber cost to find a leak? Leak detection typically runs $150–400; many plumbers waive the detection fee if they do the repair. For suspected slab leaks, hire a specialist with acoustic detection equipment rather than a general plumber.
