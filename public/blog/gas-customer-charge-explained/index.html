Gas Customer Charge Explained (Why You Pay Even With Low Usage)

Margaret Harrington December 26, 2025 8 min read

The gas customer charge is a fixed $8-$25 monthly fee you pay no matter how little gas you use. What it covers, state ranges, and how to reduce it.

Quick Answer

The gas customer charge is a fixed monthly fee — typically $8 to $27 — that every connected customer pays, no matter how little gas is used. It funds meter reading, billing, pipeline maintenance, and safety programs, and it's set by your state utility commission through a rate case, not by the utility's discretion. Low-income discount programs and LIHEAP can reduce it for qualifying households.

Why that July bill still costs money.

  • It's not a penalty — it's the base cost of staying connected to the gas system, billed 12 months a year.
  • It's regulated — the amount comes from your state PUC; the utility can't waive it on request.
  • Don't confuse it — a customer charge is a fixed line item; a minimum bill is a floor on the total.

What the Gas Customer Charge Is

The gas customer charge is a fixed monthly fee — typically $12 to $27 for residential service — that appears on your bill every month, even when your gas usage drops to zero. It is not a penalty for low usage or a billing error; it is the base cost of staying connected to the gas system. The charge covers the basics of keeping your meter, pipe connection, and account active year-round, which is why it shows up on summer bills when you might be using only a handful of therms.

Because the fee is fixed and not tied to consumption, it does not change whether you heat your home heavily in January or barely use gas in July. The amount is set by your state utility commission, not by the gas company itself, and it applies uniformly to every customer on the same rate class — so your neighbor with the same utility pays the same customer charge regardless of how much gas either of you uses.

Understanding that this line item is a flat, regulated fee is the first step before looking at what it actually pays for.

What the Charge Pays For

The customer charge isn’t a mystery line item — it pays for the fixed pieces of gas service that exist whether you use gas or not. Utilities typically break the fee into four parts. Roughly 25% goes to meter maintenance, testing, replacement, and the monthly read. About 30% covers billing statements, payment processing, and customer service. Around 25% supports the connection infrastructure — the pipes and valves running from the main up to your property. The remaining 20% funds fixed overhead like regulatory compliance and safety inspections.

Because none of that is tied to usage, the charge shows up every month, including summer when your gas use drops near zero. So when a July statement looks high despite minimal usage, the customer charge is usually the reason. Next, let’s see how much that fee actually runs by state and utility.

Customer Charges by State and Utility

The gas customer charge varies widely across the country, with typical US residential fees running from about $8 to $27 per month depending on the state and utility. To put that in concrete terms, National Grid in New York charges $22.50, ConEdison in New York charges $18.75, Peoples Gas in Illinois charges $27.00, and Nicor Gas in Illinois charges $21.00. On the lower end, CenterPoint in Minnesota charges $12.50, while PG&E in California charges $15.90 and Xcel Energy in Colorado charges $16.00. Mid-range examples include SoCalGas in California at $20.00 and Atmos Energy in Texas at $19.50, with Dominion in Ohio at $24.75. Because every utility structures its bill differently, the same fee may appear under different labels — customer charge, basic service charge, or service availability charge — so it is worth checking the charges section of your statement (see our guide to reading your gas bill) for any flat monthly fee rather than assuming it is missing. Knowing what others pay is a useful starting point for understanding whether your own charge is fair and what relief options might be available.

How the Charge Is Set and Where to Find Relief

The customer charge is set through formal rate cases at your state’s public utility commission, not negotiated directly with the gas company. As a regulated tariff item, it generally cannot be waived simply because you ask, which is why the same dollar amount appears on every bill within your rate class. That said, several relief paths do exist. Start by contacting your utility to ask whether a low-income discount, senior rate, or waiver applies to your account; some programs reduce or even eliminate the fixed charge for qualifying households. If you suspect the charge has been misapplied — for instance, if a residential account was incorrectly classified as small commercial and is carrying a higher customer charge — you can file a complaint with your state PUC. Seasonal or vacation disconnect programs offered in some states can suspend the charge during periods when a property is vacant, and federal LIHEAP assistance along with utility discount rates can further lower the effective burden for eligible low-income customers. Understanding how the charge is structured sets up a useful comparison with the other major components of your bill.

Customer Charge vs Supply, Delivery, and Minimum Bill

A natural gas bill breaks into three distinct pieces, and confusing them is the most common reason homeowners think a bill is wrong. Supply is the gas commodity itself, priced per therm, and it is the only portion that rises and falls with how much gas you actually burn. Delivery covers the distribution system that brings gas to your home and can include both fixed and usage-based components, which makes it easy to mistake for the customer charge. The customer charge is something else entirely: a single flat fee for keeping your meter, pipe connection, and account active, billed every month whether you use gas or not. It is also not the same as a minimum bill. A customer charge is a fixed line item that always appears on every connected account, while a minimum bill is a floor on the total amount owed for the billing period. Utilities that have one do not always have the other, and mixing the two up can send you to the wrong place when trying to challenge a charge — the electric-side version of this fee is covered in what is a customer charge on a utility bill.

Common Misconceptions About the Charge

The customer charge on a gas bill is not a penalty for low usage or a hidden surcharge. It is a base service fee that applies uniformly to every connected account on the same rate class, set by your state’s utility commission rather than chosen by the utility. Because the fee is fixed and tied to keeping the meter, pipe connection, and account active, it appears on every bill regardless of how much gas you actually use.

Cutting back on usage will lower the supply portion and any usage-based delivery charges, but the customer charge remains the same month after month. That is why a summer bill with near-zero therms still carries the full fee, and why trimming consumption cannot make the line item disappear. It also means the customer charge is not a useful signal when comparing bills over time — if your total gas bill is climbing, the diagnosis lives in why gas bills run high.

The cleanest month-to-month indicators are therms per day and price per therm, since those numbers reflect actual usage and rate changes. The customer charge will sit flat no matter what, so a steady customer charge between two bills is normal. If something has shifted, look at therms per day and price per therm first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a typical gas customer charge per month?

For residential gas service, the customer charge typically runs about $12 to $27 per month. Actual amounts vary by state and utility. For example, CenterPoint in Minnesota charges $12.50 while Peoples Gas in Illinois charges $27.00. Your state’s Public Utility Commission sets the exact rate.

Is the gas customer charge just a penalty fee?

No, it is not a penalty. It is a base service fee that every connected account pays, and it is the same for everyone on your rate class. It covers fixed costs like meter maintenance, billing, connection infrastructure, and regulatory compliance.

Where do I find the customer charge on my gas bill?

Look in the charges section of your statement for labels like Customer Charge, Basic Service Charge, or Service Availability Charge. It is usually listed as a flat dollar amount rather than a per-therm rate.

What's the difference between customer charge and delivery charge?

They are separate. Delivery charges cover the gas distribution system and may include both fixed and usage-based parts. The customer charge is a single fixed fee for keeping your account and connection available, and it does not vary with how much gas you use.

Can I get my gas customer charge reduced or waived?

You usually cannot negotiate the charge since your state’s Public Utility Commission sets it. You can contact your utility to ask about a low-income discount, senior rate, or waiver, file a complaint with your state PUC if misapplied, or check for budget billing to spread costs evenly.
