Utility Assistance Programs: LIHEAP, Payment Plans, and Hardship Help

Marcia Washington August 10, 2026 5 min read

Can't pay your utility bills? Here's everything you need to know about LIHEAP, utility hardship programs, payment plans, and shut-off prevention.

Quick Answer

LIHEAP (Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program) helps eligible low-income households pay home energy bills, with an average benefit of $500-800 for heating and $300-500 for cooling costs annually.

  • Fact: LIHEAP served approximately 5.3 million households in 2023, with benefits averaging $621 per household
  • Action: Apply through your local LIHEAP office - eligibility is typically 150-200% of federal poverty level

For more detail, see Understanding Your Utility Bill.

You’re staring at a utility bill you can’t pay. Maybe it’s $480, double what you expected. Maybe you’re between jobs, or an unexpected medical expense wiped out your buffer. The due date is looming, and you’re worried about shut-off.

You have more options than you think. Federal programs, state assistance, utility hardship plans, and non-profit organizations can help. This guide explains every assistance program, who qualifies, how to apply, and exactly what to do if you’re facing shut-off this week.

Emergency: Facing Shut-Off This Week

If your shut-off notice is dated within the next 7 days, take these steps NOW:

Today (Day 1)

  1. Call your utility’s customer service

    • Say: “I received a shut-off notice. I need to speak with a hardship representative about payment arrangements.”
    • Ask for: payment plan options, hardship program eligibility, medical exemption forms (if applicable), shut-off extension

  2. Document the call

    • Rep name, case number, what they promised, follow-up date

  3. Call 211

    • United Way hotline connects you with local charities that pay utility bills

  4. Check for LIHEAP emergency funding

    • Some states offer “crisis assistance” for households with shut-off notices

Success rate: 92% of shut-offs are prevented when customers call and arrange payment plans.

LIHEAP: Federal Energy Assistance

LIHEAP (Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program) is the largest federal utility assistance program. In 2025, it helped 5.7 million households pay heating and cooling bills.

What LIHEAP Covers

Expense TypeCovered?Notes
Heating billsYesElectric, gas, oil, propane, wood, kerosene
Cooling billsYesIn most states (AC/air conditioning)
Crisis assistanceYesEmergency repairs, shut-off prevention
WeatherizationYesSome states offer home upgrades

Benefit amounts (2026):

Household SizeIncome Limit (Monthly)Typical Benefit
1 person$1,617$300–$500
2 people$2,185$400–$700
3 people$2,753$500–$900
4 people$3,321$600–$1,100
5 people$3,889$700–$1,300

Income limits are 150% of federal poverty level. Benefit amounts vary by state, climate, and fuel type.

How to Apply for LIHEAP

Step 1: Find your local LIHEAP office

  • Call 211 and ask for “LIHEAP application site”
  • Visit liheap.org and search by state
  • Contact your local Community Action Agency

Step 2: Gather documents

You’ll need:

  • Social Security numbers for all household members
  • Proof of income (last 30–60 days): pay stubs, Social Security award letter, unemployment benefits
  • Proof of address (utility bill, lease)
  • Current utility bills (electric, gas, oil, propane)
  • Shut-off notice (if applying for crisis assistance)

Step 3: Apply

Application MethodHow
In-personVisit your local LIHEAP office (fastest)
OnlineMany states offer online applications
PhoneSome states allow phone applications
MailDownload form, mail with documents

Processing time:

  • Regular benefits: 30–45 days
  • Crisis assistance (shut-off notice): 5–10 days

Utility Company Hardship Programs

Most utilities have their own hardship programs, separate from LIHEAP. These are often faster and less bureaucratic.

Common Utility Programs

Program TypeWhat It OffersWho Qualifies
Payment plansExtended payment time (3–12 months)Anyone behind on bills
Budget billingFixed monthly payment (smoothed over 12 months)Customers in good standing
Hardship discounts10–40% off monthly billIncome-qualified
Winter protectionNo shut-offs during winter monthsIncome-qualified or medical need
Medical exemptionsNo shut-offs for medical equipmentDoctor certification required

Weatherization Assistance Program

WAP (Weatherization Assistance Program) is a federal program that improves home energy efficiency for low-income households. It’s not bill assistance, but it reduces future bills by making your home more efficient.

What WAP Covers

ImprovementTypical SavingsPriority Level
Attic insulation15–25% on heating/coolingHigh
Wall insulation10–20% on heating/coolingHigh
Air sealing10–15% on heating/coolingHigh
Window replacement5–10% on heating/coolingMedium
HVAC repair/replacement20–40% on heating/coolingHigh

Average benefit: $350–$500 per year in reduced energy bills

Who qualifies: Households at 200% of federal poverty level or below

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get help if I'm not low-income?

Yes, but options are more limited. You can still:

  • Set up a payment plan (most utilities offer this to anyone)
  • Enroll in budget billing (smooths seasonal spikes)
  • Negotiate a hardship extension (especially if you lost a job)

—

How fast can I get help if I'm being shut off this week?

Fastest options:

  1. Call your utility — Payment plans can be set up same-day
  2. Call 211 — Can refer to charities that pay within 48–72 hours
  3. Apply for LIHEAP crisis assistance — 5–10 days if shut-off notice
  4. Contact Salvation Army or St. Vincent de Paul — 3–7 days

Do ALL of the above simultaneously.

—

Can I use LIHEAP more than once per year?

Regular LIHEAP: Once per heating season (and once per cooling season in some states)

Crisis LIHEAP: Multiple times if you have multiple shut-off notices, but total annual benefit is capped

—

Will applying for assistance affect my credit?

No. LIHEAP, utility hardship programs, and charity assistance do not appear on credit reports and do not affect your credit score.
